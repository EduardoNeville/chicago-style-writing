# Abbreviations

Source: CMOS ch 10 (rules 10.1-10.78). Rule numbers in square brackets are CMOS 18th ed. rule numbers.
Parts: ch10. Every claim here carries its rule number.

## Apply these first

Ranked by frequency x harm for drafting and editing ordinary prose.

### From ch10

1. **[10.3]** Don't abbreviate in running prose. Spell out on first occurrence, give the abbreviation in
   parentheses, and reuse it **≥5 times** or drop it.
2. **[10.4]** Periods only when the abbreviation **ends in a lowercase letter** (*a.m., e.g., etc., ed., vol.*).
   Two or more capitals → **no periods** (*US, UK, PhD, CEO, MA, MD, NY*).
3. **[10.4]** A whole name reduced to initials loses periods and spaces (*JFK*); initials for given names keep both
   (*E. B. White*, *P. D. James*).
4. **[10.32, 10.33]** Use **two-letter postal codes** (*IL, ON*) in lists/addresses and spell state/province names
   out in text; *US* not *U.S.*.
5. **[10.34]** Commas on both sides of a state after a city (*Bedford, PA, and Jamestown, NY*), but **no comma
   before the ZIP code**.
6. **[10.10]** Pick *a/an* by **sound** (*an HMO, a UFO, an NAACP convention, an HIV test*).
7. **[10.14, 10.21]** *Jr.*, *Sr.*, *III* are part of the name: full name only, kept with titles, never after the
   surname alone.
8. **[10.15, 10.18]** Abbreviate titles only before full names (*Rep. Lauren Underwood* / *Representative
   Underwood*); drop social titles in prose and **never** use both a title and a degree (*Jennifer James, MD*).
9. **[10.23, 10.24]** Degrees and designations: no periods, lowercase spelled out, **set off by commas**
   (*Ariel Z. Lee, JD, attended . . .*).
10. **[10.46]** *a.m./p.m.* lowercase with periods; **never** with *morning/afternoon/evening/night/o'clock*.
11. **[10.47]** Time zones in parentheses after a time, capitalized when spelled out (*4:45 p.m. (CST)*).
12. **[10.43]** Only *AD* (and *AH, AM, AS*) **precede** the year (*AD 1066*); *BC, BCE, BP, CE* and the rest
    **follow** it (*753 BCE, 500 CE*); full caps, no periods, and no small caps.
13. **[10.44, 10.45]** Abbreviated months/days take periods (*Jan., Sept., Tues.*) in formal prose.
14. **[10.48]** Keep scholarly abbreviations (*e.g., i.e., etc., cf., ibid., et al.*) in **parentheses or notes**;
    never *cf.* for plain "see"; never *ed. by*; *et al.* for people, *etc.* for things.
15. **[10.7, 10.8]** Lowercase the spelled-out referent unless it's a proper name (*SARS* = severe acute
    respiratory syndrome); roman for Latin (*ibid., e.g.*), italic only for titles (*OED*).
16. **[10.12, 10.29]** No spaces around an ampersand inside an initialism (*R&D, Texas A&M, M&A*).
17. **[10.5]** No spaces inside an initialism (*C-SPAN, YMCA*); spaces between separately abbreviated words
    (*Gov. Gen., Dist. Atty.*).
18. **[10.50]** Bible books spelled out in text, abbreviated with periods in citations (*Gen. 47:12, 1 Tim. 4:6*).
19. **[10.55, 10.58, 10.64]** Scientific units: no periods, no plural *s*, space between number and unit
    (*22 m*, *36°C*), and keep quantities between 0.1 and 1,000 (*12 km*).
20. **[10.41, 10.74]** Use primes *′ ″* for feet/inches and minutes/seconds of arc — never quotation marks.
21. **[10.71]** Never start a sentence with a mathematical symbol; recast or add the term in apposition.
22. **[10.11]** Abbreviations **may** start a sentence (*NASA*, *Rep.*) — numerals may not (9.5).
23. **[10.39, 10.40]** Compass points: no periods generally (*NW*), but single-letter address forms take one
    (*E. Prospect Ave.*); never abbreviate a compass word that names the street (*South Ave.*).
24. **[10.6, 10.9]** Prefer full capitals for acronyms (*NASA, NAFTA*), follow the dictionary for lexicalized
    lowercase forms (*laser, scuba*), and **avoid small caps**.

## Overview (10.1–10.12)

- **[10.1] Where to look** — Use this chapter for general and scholarly abbreviation practice; for anything not
  listed, consult *Merriam-Webster* (7.1) and *Acronyms, Initialisms & Abbreviations Dictionary*; for technical
  work, *The CSE Manual*. **[JUDGMENT — reference lookup]**
- **[10.2] Acronym vs. initialism vs. contraction** — Three terms: an **acronym** is read as a word (*AIDS, laser,
  NASA, scuba*); an **initialism** is read letter by letter (*IRS, NBA, XML*); a **contraction** keeps the first and
  last letters of the full word (*Mr., amt.* — no period, see 10.4). Hybrids exist (*JPEG*) and the line is fuzzy
  (*FAQ*), so CMOS uses **abbreviation** as the umbrella term for all three plus shortened forms (*ibid., vol., prof.*).
  **[JUDGMENT]** (the classification only matters because it predicts article choice and periods).
- **[10.3] When to abbreviate at all** — Outside science and technology, abbreviations belong in **tabular matter,
  notes, bibliographies, and parenthetical references** — not in running prose. Two tiers:
  1. expressions **almost always abbreviated** may be used in prose without spelling out first, if Merriam-Webster
     lists them as main entries (*ATM, DIY, DNA, GPS, HMO, HTML, IQ, JPEG, laser, Ms., NASA*);
  2. merely common ones (*CIA, FDA, HVAC, MLA*) **should be spelled out at first occurrence**, at least in formal text.
  > "The use of less familiar abbreviations should be limited to terms that occur frequently enough to warrant
  > abbreviation—roughly five times or more within an article or chapter—and the terms must be spelled out on
  > their first occurrence."
  Also: **don't introduce an abbreviation you never use again** ("should not be offered only once"), and **monitor
  the total count** in a document (offer a list per 1.49, 2.27). **[JUDGMENT + MECHANICAL]** (a repeat audit is
  mechanical; the 5-use threshold is the test). Placement is flexible:
  - ex (parenthetical after the term): "Among recent recommendations of the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) are . . ."
  - ex (parenthetical before, for acronym-first): "The benefits of ERISA (Employee Retirement Income Security Act) are familiar to many."
  - ex (appositive with *or*): "The debate over genetically modified organisms, or GMOs, is by no means limited to the United States."
  - ex (abbreviate on second mention): "The US Food and Drug Administration was established in 1906. Since its inception, the FDA has . . ."
- **[10.4] Periods with abbreviations — the four-rule core** — In nontechnical settings:
  1. **Use periods** for abbreviations ending in a lowercase letter: *p., vol., e.g., i.e., etc., a.k.a., a.m., p.m., Ms.,
     Dr., et al.* (*et* is not an abbreviation; *al.* is). Exception: degrees ending in a lowercase letter (*DLitt, DMin*, see 10.23).
  2. **Use periods** for initials standing for given names (*E. B. White*), but **no periods** when a whole name is
     replaced by initials (*JFK*).
  3. **Use no periods** with abbreviations containing **two or more capital letters**, even with lowercase letters
     present: *VP, CEO, MA, MD, PhD, UK, US, NY, IL*.
  4. In publications using **traditional state abbreviations**, periods are used (*U.S., N.Y., Ill.*) — but Chicago
     **recommends two-letter postal codes (hence US)** wherever abbreviations are used (see 10.32, 10.33, 14.175).
  Cross-language: British and French omit periods from contractions (*Dr, assn, Mme*); a slash sometimes replaces
  periods (*c/o, n/a*) but more often means *per* (6.116); units of measure are spelled out in nontechnical settings.
  **18th-ed. practice:** go period-free for *US, UK, PhD, CEO, MA, NY*; keep periods on lowercase-ending forms
  (*a.m., e.g., etc., ed., vol.*) and on initials that stand for given names (*J. R. R. Tolkien* keeps periods and
  spaces; *JFK*, a whole name replaced, keeps neither). **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[10.5] Spaces inside abbreviations** — **No space** between the letters of initialisms/acronyms, lower- or
  uppercase (*RN, YMCA, C-SPAN*). **Space** is normally kept between separately abbreviated words (*Gov. Gen.,
  Mng. Ed., Dist. Atty.*), *unless* an abbreviated word is combined with a single-letter abbreviation (*S.Dak.*;
  military *S. Sgt.*). Personal names: 10.14. **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[10.6] Capitals vs. lowercase for acronyms/initialisms** — Default is **all capitals**, even when not derived
  from proper nouns (*ASAP, NATO, HIV, VP, LCD, LOL*). **Chicago generally prefers the all-capital form**
  (*NAFTA, UNICEF, SARS, COVID-19*) over the British initial-cap style (*Nafta, Unicef, Sars, Covid-19*) — unless
  Merriam-Webster lists the term lowercase (*laser, scuba*). In the sciences, follow established mixed/lower forms
  (*mRNA, IgG, bp, SARS-CoV-2*). In fiction and social-media-suffused prose, lowercase is the author's call
  (*lol, covid* or *Covid*). **[MECHANICAL]** (dictionary-checkable).
- **[10.7] Case of the spelled-out form** — Capitalize the spelled-out words **only if they would be capitalized
  anyway**; lowercase an ordinary descriptive phrase even when printed next to the all-caps abbreviation.
  - ex: *North American Free Trade Agreement (NAFTA)*; *KFC (Kentucky Fried Chicken)*
  - ex: *severe acute respiratory syndrome (SARS)*; *OCD (obsessive-compulsive disorder)*; *TGIF (thank God it's Friday; 8.92)*
  **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[10.8] Italic vs. roman** — Italicize an abbreviation **only if its spelled-out referent would be italic** — a
  book or periodical title. All common Latin abbreviations stay **roman** (*ibid., etc., e.g.*; see 7.57).
  - ex: *OED* (*Oxford English Dictionary*); *JAMA* (*Journal of the American Medical Association*)
  **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[10.9] Small caps** — **Chicago does not generally recommend** small caps for acronyms/initialisms. If a
  publisher insists: limit them to abbreviations in running text, **never** to two-letter postal codes in
  notes/bibliographies or to Roman numerals after names, and verify after file conversion that they haven't
  reverted to lowercase or full caps. **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[10.10] *a / an / the* before an abbreviation** — Choose *a* or *an* by **how the abbreviation is read aloud**,
  not by its first letter. Acronyms (read as words) rarely take an article except adjectivally.
  - ex: *an HMO, a UFO, an NAACP convention, an NBA coach, an HIV test, an MS symptom*; *a NATO member*;
    *a AA battery* (read "double A"); *a MS by . . .* (read "manuscript")
  - ex: *member nations of NATO* but *a NATO initiative*; *the NATO meeting*
  Definite article with initialisms follows **established usage** (*the NBA, the NAACP* but *W3C, PBS, NATO*);
  if usage is unsettled, use *the* only if you would use it with the spelled-out form (*DIY* takes none).
  **[MECHANICAL]** (pronunciation logic; article presence is judgment, article *form* is pattern-testable).
- **[10.11] Abbreviations at sentence start** — Unlike numerals (9.5), an abbreviation **may begin a sentence**
  without being spelled out (*NASA was created in response to the launch of Sputnik 1.*; *Rep. Robin Kelly was
  elected to her first term in 2013.*). Mathematical symbols should usually not (10.71). **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[10.12] Ampersands inside initialisms** — Leave **no space** on either side of an *&* within an initialism
  (*R&D, Texas A&M*). See also 10.29. **[MECHANICAL]**

## People and Organizations (10.13–10.31)

### Personal Names, Titles, and Degrees (10.13–10.24)

- **[10.13] Given names** — **Do not abbreviate given names** in text; the one exception is a **signature**, which
  is transcribed as written (*Benj. Franklin, Geo. D. Fuller, Ch. Virolleaud*). **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[10.14] Initials for given names** — Initials take a **period and a space**: *Roger W. Shugg, P. D. James,
  M. F. K. Fisher*. Use the period even when the middle initial stands for no name (*Harry S. Truman*). Follow the
  bearer for stage names (*LL Cool J*). If an **entire** name is reduced to initials, **omit spaces and periods**:
  *FDR, MJ, JLo* — but keep a styled form as styled (*J.Lo*, the album title). **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[10.15] Titles before names** — Civil and military titles **may be abbreviated before a full name**; before a
  **surname alone they must be spelled out**.
  - ex: *Rep. Lauren Underwood* → *Representative Underwood* (**not** ~~Rep. Underwood~~); *Sen. Kirsten E.
    Gillibrand* → *Senator Gillibrand*; *Vice Adm. Sara A. Joyner* → *Vice Admiral Joyner* **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[10.16] Civil titles** — Abbreviate only "where space is tight": *Ald., Assoc. Prof., Asst. Prof., Atty. Gen.,
  Fr.* (father), *Gov., Insp. Gen., Judge Adv. Gen., Pres., Prof., Sr.* (sister), *Supt.* **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[10.17] Military titles** — Official US military abbreviations take **no periods** and **vary by branch**
  (army *SSG*, air force/marines *SSgt*). In **general contexts and military history, use the traditional
  abbreviations** (which do not vary across branches): *Adm., Brig. Gen., Capt., Cmdr., Col., Gen., 2nd Lt.,
  Lt. Col., Lt. Gen., Maj., Maj. Gen., Sgt., S. Sgt., Warrant Officer*. Where no traditional abbreviation fits
  before a name, **use the full form**. **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[10.18] Social titles** — Social titles are **always abbreviated**, before full name or surname alone:
  *Ms., Mr., Mrs., Mx., Messrs., Dr.* The spelled-out form is only for nameless direct address (*Thank you,
  Doctor.*). But social titles are **routinely omitted in most prose**; and where a degree or professional
  designation follows the name they are **always omitted** — *Jennifer James, MD* (**not** ~~Dr. Jennifer James, MD~~).
  *Esq.* is archaic, follows a **full name only**, and never follows *Mr.* or *Dr.* **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[10.19] French social titles** — Preserve the period pattern: *M., MM.* (periods) but *Mme, Mlle* (contractions,
  **no** period). Spelled out and (in French) lowercased when used without a name. **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[10.20] “Reverend” and “Honorable”** — Abbreviate before a **full name** only when *the* does not precede; with *the*,
  spell out: *Rev. Sam Portaro* / *the Reverend Sam Portaro*; *Hon. Henry M. Brown* / *the Honorable Henry M. Brown*.
  With a surname alone, usually omit: *Birkholz* (informal "Reverend Schaefer" is tolerated). **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[10.21] “Junior,” “Senior,” and the like** — These are **part of the name**: keep them with any title or honorific, and use
  them **only with the full name, never the surname alone** — *Jordan Balfence Jr. spoke first. After Mr. Balfence
  relinquished the podium . . .*; *Zayd Zephyr III, MBA, spoke last.* Exception: in a biography about a father and
  son sharing a name, *Henry Jr.* / *Henry Sr.* with the first name alone is fine. **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[10.22] Saints** — *St.* (pl. *SS.*) before a Christian saint's name; **spell out in formal prose**, abbreviate
  only where space is at a premium, and be consistent (*Saint* or *St. Teresa*; *Saints* or *SS. Francis of Paola
  and Francis of Sales*). When *St.* is part of the name, follow the bearer (*Augustus Saint-Gaudens; Muriel St.
  Clare Byrne*). **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[10.23] Academic degrees** — **Omit periods**: *BA, DDS, MA, MD, PhD, MBA, JD, MFA, MSN, PharmD, PsyD, LLB
  (LL.B.), LLM (LL.M.), DMin, DLitt*. Lowercase the spelled-out forms in prose ("bachelor of arts", not
  "Bachelor of Arts"), even though institutions capitalize them. **Set degrees off by commas after a name**:
  *Ariel Z. Lee, JD, attended the University of Chicago Law School.* **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[10.24] Professional, religious, and other designations** — Same pattern as degrees (no periods, comma-set):
  *CNM, FAIA, FRS, LAc, LPN, MP, OFM, OP, RN, SJ* — *Jean Smith, LPN, will be working on the second floor.*
  Lowercase spelled-out designations unless they are the proper name of an organization. **[MECHANICAL]**

### Abbreviations Related to Identity (10.25–10.27)

- **[10.25] Race and ethnicity** — Capitalize the abbreviation (*AAPI, APIDA, BIPOC, POC*); lowercase the spelled-out
  phrase except for terms otherwise capitalized (*Black, Indigenous*). Usage is "generally best left to the author";
  editors **query inconsistencies** and spell out unfamiliar forms on first use. **[JUDGMENT]**
- **[10.26] Gender identity and sexual orientation** — Capitalize the abbreviation (*LGBT, LGBTQ, LGBTQIA+, AFAB,
  AMAB*), lowercase the spelled-out words; author's choice governs, editor queries inconsistencies. Note the
  lowercase exceptions already lexicalized: *ace, aro*. **[JUDGMENT]**
- **[10.27] Health and disability status** — Capitalize (*ADD, ADHD, ID, IDD, OCD, PTSD*); lowercase spelled-out
  forms; watch homographs — *AD* alone means *Alzheimer's disease* (8.145). **[JUDGMENT]**

### Companies and Other Organizations (10.28–10.31)

- **[10.28] Generic firm abbreviations** — Periods by 10.4: *Assoc., Bros., Co., Corp., Inc., Ltd., Mfg., Rwy.* or
  *Ry.* alongside period-free initialisms *LLC, LLP, LP, PLC, RR*. Non-English contractions take no period
  (*Cia* = Sp. compania; *Cie* = Fr. compagnie). **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[10.29] Company names and ampersands** — In notes, bibliographies, and tables, abbreviations and ampersands are
  fine (*Ginn & Co., JPMorgan Chase & Co., Moss Bros., RAND Corp.*). **In running text give full forms**; *Inc.*,
  *& Co.*, and *LLC* may be **omitted unless relevant** (*Johnson & Johnson was founded in 1886*). Organizations that
  are initialisms take **no periods** (10.4 rule 3), except a clear established preference (*R.E.M.*). Verify
  contemporary company names at a corporate site. **[JUDGMENT + MECHANICAL]**
- **[10.30] Media call letters** — Always capitalized, **never periods**: *ABC, CBS, HBO, KFTV, MTV, NBC, TBS,
  WFMT, WTTW*. **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[10.31] Agencies and associations** — Full capitals, **no periods**, preferably spelled out on first occurrence:
  *AAUP, AFL-CIO, EPA, EU (European Union), HMO (pl. HMOs), OECD, OPEC, WHO, WTO (formerly GATT)*. Article use
  follows 10.10. **[MECHANICAL]**

## Geographic Terms (10.32–10.41)

- **[10.32] US states and territories** — In running text **spell out** state names standing alone, and **preferably
  also after a city name** (except *DC*): "Lake Bluff, Illinois, was incorporated in 1895." Abbreviate only in
  bibliographies, tables, lists, mailing addresses — and there use **two-letter postal codes** (*AK, AL, AR, AZ, CA,
  CO, CT, DC, DE, FL, GA, HI, IA, ID, IL, IN, KS, KY, LA, MA, MD, ME, MI, MN, MO, MS, MT, NC, ND, NE, NH, NJ, NM,
  NV, NY, OH, OK, OR, PA, PR, RI, SC, SD, TN, TX, UT, VA, VT, WA, WI, WV, WY*, plus *AS, GU, MP, FM, MH, PW, VI*).
  Traditional forms exist (*Alas., Calif., Colo., Conn., Del., Fla., Ga., Ill., Ind., Kans., Ky., La., Mass., Md.,
  Mich., Minn., Miss., Mo., Mont., Neb. or Nebr., Nev., N.H., N.J., N.Mex., N.Y., N.C., N.Dak., Okla., Ore. or Oreg.,
  Pa., P.R., R.I., S.C., S.Dak., Tenn., Tex., Va., Vt., Wash., W.Va., Wis. or Wisc., Wyo.*) but Chicago prefers the
  postal codes. **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[10.33] Canadian provinces and territories** — Spell out in text ("Kingston, Ontario, is worth a visit"); use
  two-letter postal abbreviations in bibliographies and the like, since they work in English and French: *AB, BC,
  MB, NB, NL, NS, NT, NU, ON, PE, QC, SK, YT*. Keep **"the Yukon"** (author's call, query inconsistencies) and
  **always "the Northwest Territories."** **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[10.34] Comma with city plus state abbreviation** — Enclose the state/province/territory in **commas** after a city, whether
  spelled out or abbreviated: *Bedford, PA, and Jamestown, NY*. **No comma between the postal code and the ZIP
  code**: *Send the package to J. Sprocket, 3359 Fob Dr., Quartz, IL 60000.* For the term itself, USPS writes "ZIP
  Code"; "ZIP code" is also seen; **lowercase "zip code" is appropriate in most contexts** (first-listed in
  Merriam-Webster). See also 6.17, 6.42. **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[10.35] “Fort,” “Mount,” and “Saint”** — Spell the generic element out in formal prose and in mailing
  addresses; abbreviate only for space or to reflect predominant usage (*Fort (Ft.) Myers; Mount (Mt.) Airy; Port
  (Pt.) Arthur; Saint (St.) Louis* — "usually St. Louis"; "usually St. Paul"). **Never abbreviate *San* or *Santa***
  (*San Diego, Santa Barbara*). **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[10.36] Country names** — Spell out in text; abbreviate only in tables and lists, and define abbreviations in a
  table note if confusion is possible: *Fr., Ger., Isr., It., Neth., Russ., Sp., Swed.* Standard initialisms are
  fine in text after the full form: *UAE, US, UK, USSR, GDR* (or *DDR*), *FRG* (or *BRD*). ISO 3166-1 alpha-2/alpha-3
  codes are for technical applications only. **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[10.37] “US” versus “United States”** — Traditionally *United States* was the noun and *US* the adjective. **Chicago
  now permits *US* as a noun** ("subject to editorial discretion and provided the meaning is clear from context").
  - ex: *US dollars*; *US involvement in China*; *China's involvement in the United States* or *China's involvement in the US*
  **[JUDGMENT]** (18th-ed. liberalization).
- **[10.38] Mailing addresses** — USPS style is **all caps, no periods** (*AVE, PO BOX, BLDG, RD, RM, DR, RTE,
  EXPY, HWY, LN, PKWY, PL, SQ, ST, STE, TER*); Chicago's preferred tabular style uses periods (*Ave., Bldg., Rd.,
  Rm., Dr., Rte., Expy., Hwy., Ln., Pkwy., Pl., Sq., St., Ste. or Suite, Ter. or Terr.*). **In running text, spell
  out rather than abbreviate.** **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[10.39] Compass points in addresses** — **Single-letter** compass points take a **period** (*1060 E. Prospect
  Ave.*); **two-letter** ones do not (*456 NW Lane St.*). In an address, *NE, NW, SE, SW* **stay abbreviated even in
  running text**, with **no comma** before them (*I stayed in a building on N Street SW*). A compass point that is
  part of a street or place name **must never be abbreviated** (*South Ave., Northwest Hwy., South Shore Dr., West
  Bend, East Orange*). **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[10.40] Compass points generally** — Abbreviate **without periods**: *N, E, S, W, NE, SE, SW, NW, NNE, ENE, ESE*;
  *N by NE, NE by N, NE by E*. **In formal, nontechnical text, spell them out.** **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[10.41] Latitude and longitude** — In nontechnical contexts *latitude* and *longitude* are **never abbreviated**
  in running text or standing alone (*longitude 90° west*; *the polar latitudes*). In technical work *lat* and
  *long* may be used, usually without periods, and can sometimes be dropped (*lat 42°15′09″ N, long 89°17′45″ W*;
  *19°29′59″ N, 107°45′36″ W*). Give latitude first; the comma is often omitted; a minus sign means south/west.
  Use **primes (′/″), never apostrophes or quotation marks**, for minutes and seconds. **[MECHANICAL]**

## Designations of Time (10.42–10.47)

- **[10.42] Cross-references** — Units of time: 10.76. Numerals in dates/times: 9.32, 9.35, 9.37, 9.39–42.
  (No independent rule content.)
- **[10.43] Chronological eras** — **Full capitals, no periods** (Chicago's recommendation, replacing traditional
  small caps). **Precede** the year: *AD, AH, AM, AS*; **follow** it: *AUC, BC, BCE, BP, CE, KYA, MYA, YBP*.
  - ex: *AD 1066*; *753 BCE*; *753 AUC*; *AM* (*anno mundi*) is not *a.m.* (10.46)
  **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[10.44] Months** — Three systems; **in formal prose Chicago prefers the first** (periods): *Jan., Feb., Mar.,
  Apr., May, June, July, Aug., Sept., Oct., Nov., Dec.* (the period-free *Jan/Feb/Mar* set is for computer systems;
  the two-letter *Ja/F/Mr/Ap/My/Je/Jl/Ag/S/O/N/D* set is for periodical indexes). **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[10.45] Days of the week** — **Chicago recommends the first** (periods): *Sun., Mon., Tues., Wed., Thurs., Fri.,
  Sat.* (period-free *Sun/Mon/Tue/Wed/Thu/Fri/Sat* for computer code; *Su/M/Tu/W/Th/F/Sa* for indexes). **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[10.46] Time of day** — **Lowercase with periods: *a.m.* and *p.m.*** (small caps or all caps appear in some
  houses) — never with *morning, afternoon, evening, night,* or *o'clock*: *10:30 a.m.* or *ten thirty in the morning*;
  *11:00 p.m.* or *eleven o'clock at night*, **not** ~~10:30 a.m. in the morning~~. See 9.39, 9.41, 7.96. **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[10.47] Time zones** — Give the zone **in parentheses after the time**: *4:45 p.m. (CST)*. Abbreviations:
  *GMT, BST, NDT, AST, EST, EDT, CST, CDT, MST, MDT, PST, PDT, AKST, AKDT, HST, HDT*. **18th-ed. change:** "In a
  departure from previous editions, time zones are capitalized when spelled out in full" (8.91) — *Eastern Standard
  Time*, *Central Daylight Time*. Note that GMT has been superseded by *UTC* (or *UT*), though GMT references
  remain widespread. **[MECHANICAL]**

## Scholarly Abbreviations (10.48–10.49)

- **[10.48] Scholarly abbreviations** — These belong to **bibliographic references, glossaries, and scholarly
  apparatus**; some survive only for reading older texts.
  > "In formal prose, Chicago prefers to confine such abbreviations to parentheses or notes."
  Where two forms exist (*L.* and *Lat.* for Latin), the choice depends on writer, context, and readership —
  **"if in doubt, choose the longer form."** **Latin abbreviations are roman** (7.57); *ab, ad, et,* and other Latin
  words that are complete words take **no periods** (*et al.* = *et* + *al.*). Key entries, compressed:
  - *cf.* "compare; see, by way of comparison" — **not a synonym for *see***
  - *e.g.* "for example" — not to be confused with *i.e.* (6.54); *i.e.* "that is"
  - *et al.* "and others" (**no period after *et***), normally of persons; *etc.* "and so forth," of things
  - *ibid.* (13.37), *idem* (13.38), *loc. cit.* and *op. cit.* (**"best avoided"**, 13.39)
  - *q.v.* "which see," used **after** the term cross-referenced; *s.v.* (pl. *s.vv.*) used **before** it (14.130)
  - *n.d.* "no date"; *n.p.* "no place / no publisher / no page"; *ca.* or *c.* "circa" (**ca. preferred for clarity**)
  - *ed.* (pl. *eds.*) — **never add *by***: "ed. Jane Doe" or "edited by Jane Doe"; *eds.* only **after** two or more names
  - *rev.*, *trans.*, *comp.* — same no-*by* rule
  - *f.* "and the following one"; *ff.* "and the following ones" (14.48)
  - *NB* — "capitals are illogical but often used for emphasis"
  - *vs.* "versus" — **in legal contexts use *v.***; *PPS* "a later postscript"; *floruit* (*fl.*) for unknown dates
  - German-text forms keep a **space between initials**: *d. h.* (das heißt), *d. i.* (das ist)
  **Not in this glossary:** *passim* and *sic* — see 7.57 (*sic* is always italic) for their treatment.
  **[MECHANICAL]** (each entry is a lookup/pattern; choosing among *ibid./op. cit.* is judgment per ch 13).
- **[10.49] Scholarly symbols** — Use the symbols where they replace the abbreviation: *©* copyright; *=* "equals,
  the same as"; *¶* (pl. *¶¶*) paragraph; *§* (pl. *§§*) section. **[MECHANICAL]**

## Biblical Abbreviations (10.50–10.54)

  Ephesians constitute a sermon on love*; *Jeremiah, chapters 42–44, records the flight . . .*; *According to
  Genesis 1:27 . . .*). **Abbreviate in parenthetical citations, notes, or where references are dense**: *Gen.
  47:12, Ruth 4:15, Isa. 44:14, Acts 7:21, and 1 Tim. 4:6.* **[MECHANICAL]**

  *1 Mc*; *2 Macc.* or *2 Mc*; *Pr. of Man.*; *Sir.* or *Sir*; *Sus.*; *Tob.* or *Tb*; *Ws*; *Wisd. of Sol.*

## Technology and Science (10.55–10.65)

- **[10.55] Technical abbreviations** — Governing rules, then samples only (the list runs pp. 651–655): **periods
  are omitted in any context** (contrast 10.4); **plurals do not add *s*** (*10 A, 5 ha*); a **space separates
  numeral and unit** except with degree symbols (*22 m* but *36°C*); spelled-out forms are **lowercase except proper
  nouns**; symbols derived from proper names keep a capital (*A, V, Wb,* the *C* in *°C*), as do prefix letters
  *M, G, T*. Representative entries: *bp* (boiling point; base pair), *cc* / *cm³* (cubic centimeter, clinical vs.
  scientific), *dB, DOI, DVD, GIS, HTTP, HTTPS, Hz, IP, IR, KB/kB, kg, km, kW, L* (capitalized to avoid
  confusion with *1*), *Mb/MB, mg, mL, mpg, mph, ms, nm, OCR, OS, PC, PDF, pH, ppb, ppm, RAM, ROM, rpm, UV,
  URL, USB, VoIP, XML*. Also: *kg* is the base unit but prefixes attach to *gram* (10.61); *km/h* with a slash is SI
  style. **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[10.56] Statistical abbreviations** — Use in statistical text and tables; "sometimes italicized": *ANOVA,
  ANCOVA, CI, CL, CLT, df* (also *DF, dof*), *GLM, HR, IQR, LS, MLE, MS, N, n, ns, OLS, OR, p, r, R², RMS, sd* or
  *SD, se* or *SE, sem* or *SEM, SS, SSE, SST, WLLN*, plus the symbols for the mean value and the chi-square
  distribution. **[MECHANICAL]**- **[10.57] SI overview** — SI = the expanded metric system; for current detail consult BIPM's *The International
  System of Units* and *The CSE Manual*. **[JUDGMENT — reference]**
- **[10.58] SI form** — **No periods** after SI symbols; **the same symbol serves singular and plural**; most symbols
  are lowercase, with capitals for units derived from proper names (*A* for ampere) and for forms that must be
  distinguished from lookalikes; spelled-out unit names are lowercase except *Celsius*-type names that follow
  "degree." **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[10.59] SI plurals** — The symbol never changes, but read/prose forms pluralize: *3 m* = *three meters*, yet
  *a three-meter span*; quantities ≠ 1 read plural (*0.5 m* = "zero point five meters"; *a zero-point-five-meter
  span*). See 9.21, 10.73. **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[10.60] SI base units** — Seven: *m* (meter), *kg* (kilogram), *s* (second), *A* (ampere), *K* (kelvin),
  *mol* (mole), *cd* (candela). Mass ≠ weight. **No degree sign with *K***. Base *quantity* symbols (*l, m, t*)
  are **italic** variables. **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[10.61] Kilogram vs. gram** — The kilogram is the base unit, but **prefixes attach to *gram***: *Mg, mg, ng*,
  etc. **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[10.62] SI prefixes** — Attach prefixes to base/derived units for multiples and submultiples. Drop the final
  vowel in three cases: *kΩ* (kilohm, not ~~kiloohm~~), *MΩ* (megohm), *ha* (hectare). Full set: *yotta Y, zetta Z,
  exa E, peta P, tera T, giga G, mega M, kilo k, hecto h, deka da, deci d, centi c, milli m, micro µ, nano n,
  pico p, femto f, atto a, zepto z, yocto y* (10²⁴ → 10⁻²⁴). **Never use SI prefixes for powers of two**; binary
  multiples take *kibi-* (Ki), *mebi-* (Mi), *gibi-*, *tebi-*, *pebi-*, *exbi-* (9.12). **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[10.63] Derived units** — Express algebraically from base units (*m², m³, m/s, m/s², kg/m³*); named derived
  units (*Hz, V, W, J/K, N/m*) may substitute. Separate unit symbols in a product with a **nonbreaking space or a
  dot operator** (*N m* or *N·m*), never nothing. **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[10.64] SI spacing and magnitude** — Express a quantity with a number **between 0.1 and 1,000**: *12 km*, not
  ~~12000 m~~; *3 mm³*, not ~~0.003 cm³~~. Put a **space between numeral and symbol** — SI also wants a space
  before *%* and before a temperature degree sign, but **Chicago does not require those two exceptions**
  (*0.5 %* SI vs. *0.5%* Chicago). Firearms usage may close the space before a noun (*a 9mm Luger*) if consistent.
  **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[10.65] Non-SI units accepted** — *L* (liter), *t* (metric ton), *h* (hour) are outside SI but accepted for use
  within it. **[MECHANICAL]**

### Astronomy (10.66–10.68)

- **[10.66] Resources** — Astronomers supplement SI with field-specific notation; see the International Astronomical
  Union site and *The CSE Manual*. **[JUDGMENT — reference]**
- **[10.67] Celestial coordinates** — *RA* or *α* (right ascension) in hours/minutes/seconds of sidereal time, set as
  **superscripts**: *14ʰ6ᵐ7ˢ*. *δ* (declination) in degrees, minutes, seconds, with sign or unmarked for north and a
  minus for south: *−49°8′22″*; decimals as *14ʰ6ᵐ7.8ˢ*, *+34°.26*. **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[10.68] Other astronomical abbreviations** — *AU* or *ua* (astronomical unit), *ly* (light-year), *pc* (parsec),
  *kpc, Mpc, UT* (Universal Time). **[MECHANICAL]**

### Chemical Elements (10.69)

- **[10.69] Element names and symbols** — IUPAC governs names; each element also carries an atomic number used to
  settle naming disputes. Symbols take an **initial capital and no period**; **element names are lowercase**
  (*aluminum*/*aluminium*, *cesium*/*caesium*). Where a symbol comes from another root, give it in parentheses
  (*Sb* for antimony, *stibium*; *W* for tungsten, *wolfram*; *Hg* for mercury, *hydrargyrum*). Provisional Latin
  number-names apply to unconfirmed elements. Names of elements are never capitalized (8.150–51). **[MECHANICAL]**

### Symbols for Math (10.70–10.71)

- **[10.70] Mathematical signs and symbols** — Use the **real math character**, not a lookalike, because the math
  codepoint carries the correct spacing (6.130). Excerpt, with Unicode points: plus *+* (002B); minus *−* (2212);
  multiplication *×* (00D7); dot operator *⋅* (22C5); division *÷* (00F7), slash *∕* (2215); plus-or-minus *±*
  (00B1); union *∪* (222A), intersection *∩* (2229); logic: ∧, ∨, ¬, ⇒, →, ⇔, ↔, ∃, ∀, ⊢, ∴, ∵ (2227…2235);
  prime ′ (2032), double prime ″ (2033), degree ° (00B0); π (03C0), *e* (0065); ⊥, ∥, ∦, ∠, √ (221A), ∞ (221E),
  ∂ (2202), ∇ (2207), ∅ (2205), ! factorial (0021). **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[10.71] Don't begin a sentence with a math symbol** — Unlike acronyms (10.11), a symbol at sentence start is
  hard to see; **recast or insert the term in apposition**.
  - ex (wrong → right): *Assume that x ∈ S. ~~S is countable.~~* → *Assume that x ∈ S. The set S is countable.*
  - ex (semicolon if closely related): *A function f is even if f(−x) = f(x); f is odd if f(−x) = −f(x).*
  Separate consecutive expressions with words or punctuation: *Suppose that a = bq + r, where 0 < r < b.* **[MECHANICAL]**

### US Measure (10.72–10.76)

- **[10.72] Periods with US measure** — In scientific copy, **no periods** (*10 yd, 5 lb*); in **nonscientific
  contexts, periods are customary** (*10 yd., 5 lb.*). **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[10.73] Plurals of US measure** — Identical in singular and plural (*10 yd., 5 lb., 8 sq. mi.*), read plural
  for quantities ≠ 1 (*point five yards*; *a point-five-yard increment*). See 10.59. **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[10.74] Length, area, volume** — *in.* or *″*, *ft.* or *′*, *yd., rd., mi.; sq. in., sq. ft., cu. ft.*
  **Foot and inch take prime and double prime, not quotation marks** (*6′1″* — no space). Exponents only when
  unambiguous: *425 ft.²* = *425 sq. ft.*, not 425 ft. by 425 ft. **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[10.75] Weight and capacity** — Weight: *gr., scruple, dr., dwt., oz., lb.* or *#, cwt., tn.*; disambiguate
  systems as *lb. av.* (avoirdupois), *lb. t.* (troy), *lb. ap.* (apothecaries'). Capacity: *pt., qt., gal., bbl.,
  pk., bu.*; liquid: *min., fl. dr., fl. oz., gi.* Abbreviations **do not change in the plural** (*12 gal.,
  3 pt.*). Note US and British pints differ but share abbreviations. **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[10.76] Time units** — *sec., min., h.* or *hr., d., wk., mo., yr.* Unlike length/weight, these
  **often take an *-s* plural in nontechnical writing**: *5 secs., 12 hrs.* or *12 h., 15 yrs.* **[MECHANICAL]**

## Commercial and Social Abbreviations (10.77–10.78)

- **[10.77] Commercial terms** — Periods are **normally used in lowercased commercial abbreviations** (10.4):
  *acct., agt., a.k.a., amt., attn., bal., bbl., bdl., c/o, ctn., cttee.* or *comm., dis., dist., distr., doz.,
  dr., ea., exec., gro., inst., inv., mdse., mfg., mfr., mgmt., mer., msg., mtg., mtge., pd., pkg., qt., qty., recd.*
  or *rec'd, std., treas., ult., whsle.*; period-free initialisms where the letters are capitals: *AP, APR, AR,
  ASAP, BS, COD, COGS, COLA, CPI, CPM, DEI, DJIA, EEO, EOE, EOM, FAS, FOB, FY, GAAP, GL, GM, IPO, JIT, LBO, LIFO,
  M&A, MO, NA* or *n/a, NGO, OJT, OS, OTC, POE, POP, POS, P&H, QA, QC, R, S&H, SM, TBA, TBD, VAT*. Note *™* and
  *®* (8.154) and closed compounds written as *M&A, P&H, S&H* (no spaces around *&*). **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[10.78] Social abbreviations and emojis** — Texting/social-media initialisms are **frequently lowercased in
  actual usage** (10.6); in fiction and creative prose the choice of caps, lowercase, or initial cap is the
  author's, with the editor enforcing **consistency**: *IMHO* or *imho, LOL* or *lol, TL;DR* or *tl;dr, TTYL* or
  *ttyl*. Emoji use is likewise the author's call; the editor ensures consistent punctuation/spacing (6.137) and
  that each emoji is a **Unicode character, not an image** (convert embedded images; if no character exists, set as
  art per 2.19; if the historical rendering matters, use an image with alt text). Emojipedia.org lists Unicode
  names and code points (e.g. 🙂 U+1F60A, 🔥 U+1F525, 🙏 U+1F64F). See also 11.2. **[JUDGMENT + MECHANICAL]**
