# Source citations: notes-bibliography and author-date

Source: CMOS ch 13 (rules 13.1-13.132), ch 14 (rules 14.1-14.207). Rule numbers in square brackets are CMOS 18th ed. rule numbers.
Parts: ch13, ch14. Every claim here carries its rule number.

## Apply these first

Ranked by frequency x harm for drafting and editing ordinary prose.

### From ch13

Ranked by how often they change real prose; each is directly applicable when drafting or editing.

1. **Cite quotations, paraphrases, and non-obvious facts — nothing else** (13.1, 13.5). Every direct quotation and
   paraphrase needs a source; every generally knowable fact does not. Resist the citation-as-repository reflex.
2. **Don't over-cite; mention instead** (13.5, 13.68). Tangential sources get a mention in the text, not a note or
   bibliography entry.
3. **Pick one system and never mix** (13.2, 13.4): notes and bibliography (humanities) or author-date (sciences),
   consistently throughout.
4. **Notes separate elements with commas; bibliography and reference-list entries separate them with periods**
   (13.18, 13.102). This single contrast prevents most malformed entries.
5. **Invert only the first author in a bibliography/reference list; never invert in a note** (13.22, 13.77,
   13.78). Only the first-listed name, and only in the list.
6. **Bibliography: up to six authors, then first three + *et al.*; notes and text: first author + *et al.* as
   soon as there are more than two** (13.23, 13.78, 13.107, 13.123) — 18th-ed. thresholds, both changed from the
   17th.
7. **Omit the place of publication for books** (13.23, 14.30) — a change that breaks most 17th-ed. habits.
8. **No page range for a chapter in a bibliography or reference list; page in the note or text citation**
   (13.25, 13.109).
9. **Never use *ibid.*, *idem*, *op. cit.*, or *loc. cit.* — use a short form instead** (13.37–13.39). The short
   form is last name + short title + page. If *ibid.* is unavoidable, it may refer only to the immediately
   preceding single work.
10. **Short titles: last name + key words, unchanged word order, ≤4 words usually left whole, initial *The/A/An*
    usually dropped, typography preserved** (13.33, 13.35, 13.36).
11. **Place note numbers at the end of a sentence or clause; after any punctuation except a dash, which they
    precede** (13.29). Never inside or at the end of a chapter title (13.30).
12. **One note reference per location; several citations may share one note, separated by semicolons in text
    order** (13.31, 13.61).
13. **Italics for whole works (books, journals, newspapers, films); quotation marks for parts (chapters,
    articles, poems in a collection)** (13.88).
14. **Title case for English titles; sentence case for non-English titles** (13.89, 13.100).
15. **Insert a colon between title and subtitle even if the source has none; capitalize the subtitle** (13.91);
    with two subtitles use colon then semicolon (13.92).
16. **No colon before a subtitle when the title ends in ? or !; drop a period that would follow ? or !**
    (13.98).
17. **Use *and*, never an ampersand, between authors** (13.78); an ampersand in a quoted title may be silently
    changed to *and* (13.90).
18. **Repeat authors' names in successive bibliography entries — no 3-em dash** (13.72, 13.113); in reference
    lists, repeat names too, and order same-author works chronologically, not alphabetically by title (13.112).
19. **Author-date citations carry no punctuation between name and year; page follows a comma; multiple sources are
    separated by semicolons** (13.116, 13.117, 13.124).
20. **Don't repeat the author's name in parentheses if it is in the text — and put the date immediately after the
    name, even possessive** (13.119).
21. **Every author-date text citation must match a reference-list entry by name and date, and page references must
    fall inside the article's range** (13.115); same author + same year = a, b, c by title (13.114).
22. **In a block quotation with the source at the end, put the parenthesis after the final punctuation and use no
    period after it** (13.120).
23. **Use *cf.* only for "compare"; *see* for "consult"** (13.45). A common and conspicuous misuse.
24. **URLs last, in full, protocol lowercase even after a period, no shorteners; DOI as `https://doi.org/…`, never
    "DOI:"** (13.6–13.12). Access dates only when no publication or revision date exists (13.15).
25. **Label what you built**: *Bibliography* vs. *Works Cited* vs. *Selected Bibliography* (preferred over *Select
    Bibliography*) vs. *Suggested Readings*, with a headnote explaining selection (13.68).

### From ch14

1. **[14.1]** Default to notes-bibliography; switch to author-date by moving the year after the author's name and keeping every other element in place.
2. **[14.2]** Never omit publisher or year; never add a city of publication for a post-1900 book.
3. **[14.30–14.31]** Pre-1900 book → `(City, Year)` and no publisher; post-1900 → `(Publisher, Year)`.
4. **[14.33]** Strip `The`, `Inc.`, `Co.`, `& Co.`, `Publishing Co.` from publisher names; keep `Press` for university presses.
5. **[14.5–14.6]** `ed.`/`trans.` after the name when no author; `Edited by`/`Translated by` after the title when an author exists — and never `eds.` in that position.
6. **[14.8–14.9]** Book chapter: `"Chapter," in *Book*, ed. Editor (Publisher, Year), page.` No page range in the bibliography.
7. **[14.66]** Commas in notes, periods in bibliographies; `in` never introduces a periodical.
8. **[14.70]** Journal: `*Journal* 132, no. 3 (2022): 621–43` — volume with no punctuation, issue always recorded, year in parentheses, page after a colon.
9. **[14.71]** Notes cite the passage; bibliographies give first–last page.
10. **[14.73]** Prefer a DOI URL; drop the URL if you used print; substitute a database name for an unusable link.
11. **[14.74]** Don't add access dates unless the venue requires them.
12. **[14.87]** Magazine → date only, page after a comma, inclusive pages optional.
13. **[14.89]** Newspaper → month day, year are indispensable; add edition/section/column only if useful.
14. **[14.90]** Convert headlines to title case and periods inside headlines to colons.
15. **[14.96]** Don't create bibliography entries for ephemeral news items — cite them in text or notes.
16. **[14.104]** Web page: title, site, owner/sponsor, update date (else access date), URL — and file it under the owner/sponsor in a bibliography.
17. **[14.111–14.112]** Conversations, emails, and AI chats belong in the text or a note, with the medium, date, and (for AI) the tool version and how the output was used.
18. **[14.113]** Theses take quotation marks, not italics, plus kind, institution, and date.
19. **[14.130]** Dictionaries and encyclopedias: cite the **entry in quotation marks under the work's title**, in a note, not a bibliography.
20. **[14.160]** Don't cite "quoted in" without need — examine the original, or list both sources.
21. **[14.166]** Live performances and private documents are cited in text or notes, never as bibliography entries.
22. **[14.170–14.172]** Legal documents follow *Bluebook*/*ALWD*, appear in notes only, and are never forced into an author-date reference list.
23. **[14.47, 14.51]** Use first-and-last page ranges with en dashes and `volume:page` with no space.
24. **[14.44–14.45]** `n.d.` and `forthcoming` are the only date substitutes; both behave differently in notes and reference lists.
25. **[14.136–14.137]** For Indigenous knowledge, cite the Knowledge Keeper by name, nation, territory, topic, and date — and confirm permission before publishing.

## Master templates (the five patterns the whole chapter encodes)

These are the chapter's own patterns, abstracted from the examples at the rule numbers cited beside each
block. Use them as the default shapes; replace each placeholder, delete nothing.

**(1) Full note** — from 13.18–13.26. Note the 18th-ed. features: **no place of publication** (13.23, 14.30),
comma after the opening parenthesis of the date, page number last and bare.

```text
n. Firstname Lastname, Title of Work: Subtitle (Publisher, Year), page.
n. Firstname Lastname and Firstname Lastname, Title of Work (Publisher, Year), page.
n. Firstname Lastname, "Title of Chapter," in Title of Book, ed. Firstname Lastname (Publisher, Year), page.
n. Firstname Lastname, "Title of Article," Journal Name vol, no. issue (Year): page, https://doi.org/10.xxxx/xxxxx.
n. Firstname Lastname, ed., Title of Work (Publisher, Year), page.
```

**(2) Shortened note** — from 13.33–13.36: last name + key words of the main title + page. No `ed.`/`trans.`;
no place, publisher, or year; page repeated even when it is the same as the preceding note (13.37).

```text
n. Lastname, Short Title, page.
n. Lastname and Lastname, Short Title, page.
n. Lastname et al., Short Title, page.
n. Lastname, "Short Title," page.
```

**(3) Bibliography entry** — from 13.22–13.26, 13.66, 13.78. First (and only the first) author inverted;
elements separated by periods; no page numbers for books; full article page range after the colon.

```text
Lastname, Firstname. Title of Work: Subtitle. Publisher, Year.
Lastname, Firstname, and Firstname Lastname. Title of Work. Publisher, Year.
Lastname, Firstname, ed. Title of Work. Publisher, Year.
Lastname, Firstname. "Title of Chapter." In Title of Book, edited by Firstname Lastname. Publisher, Year.
Lastname, Firstname. "Title of Article." Journal Name vol, no. issue (Year): firstpage–lastpage. https://doi.org/10.xxxx/xxxxx.
```

**(4) Author-date reference-list entry** — from 13.106–13.110. Year immediately after the author's name;
issue number in parentheses with no `no.`; no parentheses around the year; no chapter page range.

```text
Lastname, Firstname. Year. Title of Work: Subtitle. Publisher.
Lastname, Firstname, and Firstname Lastname. Year. Title of Work. Publisher.
Lastname, Firstname, ed. Year. Title of Work. Publisher.
Lastname, Firstname. Year. "Title of Chapter." In Title of Book, edited by Firstname Lastname. Publisher.
Lastname, Firstname. Year. "Title of Article." Journal Name vol (issue): firstpage–lastpage. https://doi.org/10.xxxx/xxxxx.
```

**(5) Author-date parenthetical citation** — from 13.116–13.124. No punctuation between author and year; page
after a comma; sources separated by semicolons.

```text
(Lastname Year)
(Lastname Year, page)
(Lastname Year, page, page)                 [nonconsecutive pages, comma]
(Lastname and Lastname Year, page)
(Lastname et al. Year, page)
(Lastname Year; Lastname Year, page)        [two or more sources]
(Lastname Yeara, page)                     [same author, same year: a, b, c after the year]
(Lastname Year, 2:345)                      [volume:page]
(Lastname Year, 212n3)                      [note]
(Lastname Year, chap. 4)
```

Two structural contrasts to keep straight: notes and bibliography separate **elements with commas** (13.18);
bibliography and reference-list entries separate **elements with periods** (13.18, 13.102). Same data, two
punctuation grammars.

## Introduction to Source Citations (13.1–13.5)

- **[13.1] Purpose of source citations** — Identify the source of every **direct quotation or paraphrase** and
  of every **fact or opinion not generally known or easily checked** (see 12.1–6): "Ethics, copyright laws, and
  courtesy to readers require" it. Whatever convention is used, a citation "must always provide sufficient
  information either to lead readers directly to the sources consulted or, for materials that may not be readily
  available, to enable readers to positively identify them," print or electronic, published or not.
  **[JUDGMENT]**
- **[13.2] The two Chicago systems** — (1) **Notes and bibliography**: numbers in text → numbered footnotes or
  endnotes; sources may also be listed in a bibliography. (2) **Author-date**: parenthetical references in text
  → list of sources at the end. Both treat capitalization and punctuation identically; only the delivery differs.
  Use notes-and-bibliography for literature, history, and the arts (it is the manual's **default**, and the
  default for ch. 14 examples); use author-date for the sciences and social sciences. **[JUDGMENT]**
  - ex: notes "allow space for unusual types of sources as well as for commentary on the sources cited";
    author-date "credits researchers by name directly in the text while at the same time emphasizing the date."
- **[13.3] Other citation systems** — MLA and APA (parenthetical citations in the text keyed to a list of
  sources), AMA (a numbered list of references, cited in the text by reference number), CSE (numbered, like AMA). For legal and public documents Chicago recommends *The Bluebook*
  (see 14.170–207). Journal authors follow the journal's instructions. **[JUDGMENT]**
- **[13.4] Flexibility and consistency** — Any "logical and defensible variations" on Chicago-style citations are
  acceptable **if a consistent style is maintained within the work** and author and publisher agree. This
  flexibility "is rarely possible in journal publication," which calls for the journal's established style.
  **[JUDGMENT]**
- **[13.5] Mentioning versus citing** — In works without an academic focus, formal citations are rarely needed;
  news and other journalism rely on **mentions** ("the eighteenth edition of *The Chicago Manual of Style*" in a
  blog post needs no note). In academic work too, consider each source: tangential sources may be mentioned in
  the text but "rarely require a fuller citation."
  > "In general, readers are better served by a disciplined, focused approach to source citations than by one
  > that treats citations as a repository for every scrap of information consulted during the course of research
  > and writing." (13.5)

## URLs and Other Identifiers (13.6–13.12)

- **[13.6] URLs** — For online sources, record a URL as the **final element** of the citation, after every other
  component. Whether a URL survives into publication depends on whether readers need it to locate or assess the
  source; prefer making the **title** the hyperlink rather than printing a raw machine-readable URL.
  **[MECHANICAL]** (a bare URL belongs last; no trailing period rule change — see 13.12)
  - ex manuscript: `Hui, Andrew. "Dreams of the Universal Library." Critical Inquiry 48, no. 3 (2022): 522–48. https://doi.org/10.1086/718629.`
  - ex published: same entry ending `522–48.` with the title linked.
- **[13.7] DOIs** — A DOI-based URL is `https://doi.org/` + DOI (prefix + suffix), e.g.
  `https://doi.org/10.1086/718629`. **Avoid the deprecated form "DOI:" followed by the number** in your own
  citations. **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[13.8] Permalinks and the like** — A DOI is **always preferable**. If none is listed, use a URL labeled
  persistent link, permalink, or stable URL (often near the top of the document or behind a "cite"/"share"
  button). **Test every URL** before using it; if it is excessively long or subscription-gated, name the website
  or database instead (13.9, 13.10). **[JUDGMENT]**
- **[13.9] Editing long URLs** — A very long URL "can often be shortened simply by finding a better version of the
  link"; prefer a DOI or other stable identifier. Example: a Google Books URL pointing at one searched passage
  (`…&pg=PA226#v=onepage&q&f=false`) should give way to the book's main page (`…?id=bF81AAAAMAAJ`) when a page
  reference is already in the citation. **[JUDGMENT]**
- **[13.10] Library and bibliographic databases** — For a source available only by subscription, **name the
  database in lieu of a URL**. Test the URL while logged out: a URL that leads to information about the source
  (if not the source) is safe to use; a DOI-based URL "is supposed to direct readers to information about a
  source even when they do not have full access." **[JUDGMENT]**
- **[13.11] Link-shortening services** — **Do not** create short URLs with TinyURL, Bitly, shortDOI, or the like
  in a manuscript: short URLs "obscure domain names and other details that may be important to copyeditors."
  Publishers may shorten in the published version. **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[13.12] URLs in relation to surrounding text** — URLs contain no spaces; give them **in full, beginning with
  the protocol** (`https` or `http`). The `h` stays lowercase **even after a period**; do not edit the case of
  the rest (identifiers can be case-sensitive); retain a trailing slash; retain any period or comma that would
  follow the URL as an ordinary word. **In running text, avoid beginning a sentence with a URL.** **[MECHANICAL]**

## Keeping Track of Your Research (13.13–13.17)

- **[13.13] Citation management tools** — Use EndNote/Zotero/Word plugins rather than keying citations from
  scratch, but "the results … are only as good as the data behind the citations": (a) **double-check your data**
  field by field against the source as you acquire it (it is fine to collect more than you will cite);
  (b) **double-check your citations** in the manuscript for errant punctuation, capitalization, and missing or
  superfluous data, and fix them in the application, not just the text; (c) **back up your data**. Software
  handles books and journal articles well, "the variety of sources typically cited in a scholarly work …
  usually precludes an acceptable result from software alone" — review every citation for consistency,
  accuracy, and completeness. Save a coded backup before citations are stripped to plain text. **[JUDGMENT]**
- **[13.14] Citing the right version** — Note which version you consulted; Chicago recommends a URL to signal
  that a work was consulted online. Alternate electronic formats from a **single** publisher at the same URL
  (PDF vs. HTML) "do not need to be indicated"; but for items designed to be read on a particular application
  or device, **specify the format or device**. **[JUDGMENT]**
- **[13.15] Access dates** — Chicago **does not require** access dates in published citations "unless no date of
  publication or revision can be determined from the source." Rationale: earlier versions may be unavailable to
  readers, authors consult a source repeatedly over days or months, and the recorded date cannot be verified.
  Some publishers and most student work **do** require them — check early. **[JUDGMENT]**
- **[13.16] "Last modified" and other revision dates** — There are "no accepted standards"; for formally published
  sources a publication date matters more. Include a revision date **only** if it functions as the de facto
  publication date or is the only date available — useful chiefly for wikis and other frequently updated works.
  **[JUDGMENT]**
- **[13.17] Preserving a permanent record** — Keep a copy of any source that is **not formally published** (social
  media posts, corporate web pages, a news article reporting an ongoing crisis) — printout, screen capture, or a
  link-archiving service (Perma.cc, the Wayback Machine's Save Page Now). A journal article or a book cataloged
  by the Library of Congress is exempt. **[JUDGMENT]**

## Notes and Bibliography: Basic Format (13.18–13.20)

- **[13.18] Overview** — "In the system favored by many writers in the humanities, bibliographic citations are
  provided in notes, preferably supplemented by a bibliography." Notes are usually numbered, matching superscript
  numbers in the text. Notes are "styled much like ordinary sentences, with authors' names in normal order and
  the elements separated by commas and parentheses." If a bibliography lists everything cited, **short forms are
  acceptable in the notes even at first mention**; with no bibliography (or a selected list only) give full
  details at first mention in each chapter and short forms thereafter.
  - ex note: `1. Erica Gies, Water Always Wins: Thriving in an Age of Drought and Deluge (University of Chicago Press, 2022), 8, 27.`
  - ex short: `2. Gies, Water Always Wins, 209–10.`
  - ex bibliography: `Gies, Erica. Water Always Wins: Thriving in an Age of Drought and Deluge. University of Chicago Press, 2022.`
- **[13.19] Abbreviations: notes vs. bibliographies** — In a **note**, abbreviate *ed.* / *eds.* (editor/editors),
  *ed.* (edited by), *trans.* (translator and translated by), *vol.* / *vols.*, *ed.* / *eds.* (edition). In a
  **bibliography**, noun forms stay abbreviated but **verb forms are spelled out**: *edited by*, *translated by*.
  **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[13.20] Page numbers and other locators** — In **notes**, cite only the page(s) of the passage: a range with an
  en dash, separate locations with a comma. In **bibliographies**, give **no** page numbers for books but the
  **full beginning and ending pages** of a journal article. For unpaged electronic sources, substitute a chapter
  or paragraph number, a section heading, or "a descriptive phrase that follows the organizational divisions of
  the work"; for short searchable documents such locators are often unnecessary. **[MECHANICAL]**
  - ex: `1. … Environmental History 27, no. 3 (2022): 422.` → `2. Duan and Clements, "Fighting for Forests," 431–32.`
    → bibliography `… Environmental History 27, no. 3 (2022): 415–40.`

## Notes and Bibliography: Examples and Variations (13.21–13.26)

- **[13.21] How the examples work** — Each example gives a numbered note, its shortened form, and the
  corresponding bibliography entry. In a work whose bibliography lists every source in full, the **shortened
  form is acceptable even at first mention**. **[JUDGMENT]**
- **[13.22] Book with single author or editor** — Invert the name in the bibliography, **not** in the note; include
  page numbers in the note, **not** in the bibliography entry.
  - ex: `1. Charles Yu, Interior Chinatown (Pantheon Books, 2020), 95–96.` / `2. Yu, Interior Chinatown, 101, 103.`
    / `Yu, Charles. Interior Chinatown. Pantheon Books, 2020.`
- **[13.23] Books with multiple authors or editors** — Two authors: list both in the note (full **and** short),
  invert only the first in the bibliography.
  - ex: `1. Terry Greene Sterling and Jude Joffe-Block, Driving While Brown … (University of California Press, 2021), 110.`
    / `2. Sterling and Joffe-Block, Driving While Brown, 205–6.`
  - **18th-ed. change:** in a **note**, more than **two** authors → first author + "et al." (previous editions:
    more than three). In the **bibliography**, list up to **six** names; more than six → first **three** + "et al."
    (previously two to ten names). With no bibliography, list up to six in the first full note.
  - ex: `1. Katie Aubrecht et al., eds., The Aging-Disability Nexus (UBC Press, 2020), 44.` /
    `Aubrecht, Katie, Christine Kelly, and Carla Rice, eds. The Aging-Disability Nexus. UBC Press, 2020.`
  - Note also (13.23): "Chicago now omits the place of publication in most source citations for books (see 14.30)."
- **[13.24] Book with author plus editor or translator** — *ed.* / *trans.* in the note becomes **Edited by** /
  **Translated by** in the bibliography.
  - ex: `1. Gabriel García Márquez, Love in the Time of Cholera, trans. Edith Grossman (Alfred A. Knopf, 1988), 242–55.`
    / `García Márquez, Gabriel. Love in the Time of Cholera. Translated by Edith Grossman. Alfred A. Knopf, 1988.`
- **[13.25] Chapter in an edited book** — Give the chapter author, the chapter title in quotation marks, and the
  editor, and **put *in* before the book title**. **18th-ed. change:** it is "no longer necessary to record a
  page range for the chapter in the bibliography entry (a departure from previous editions)"; the page still
  appears in the note.
  - ex: `1. Glenn Gould, "Streisand as Schwarzkopf," in The Glenn Gould Reader, ed. Tim Page (Vintage Books, 1984), 310.`
    / `2. Gould, "Streisand as Schwarzkopf," 309.` / `Gould, Glenn. "Streisand as Schwarzkopf." In The Glenn Gould Reader, edited by Tim Page. Vintage Books, 1984.`
- **[13.26] Journal article** — Include volume and issue number and year. The **volume number follows the
  italicized journal title in roman with no intervening punctuation**; a specific page in the note, the article's
  full page range in the bibliography, **preceded by a colon**. A month or season "can usually be omitted (but
  see 14.70)."
  - ex: `1. Hyeyoung Kwon, "Inclusion Work: Children of Immigrants Claiming Membership in Everyday Life," American Journal of Sociology 127, no. 6 (2022): 1824, https://doi.org/10.1086/720277.`
    / `Kwon, Hyeyoung. "Inclusion Work: …" American Journal of Sociology 127, no. 6 (2022): 1818–59. https://doi.org/10.1086/720277.`

## Notes (13.27–13.64)

### Note Numbers (13.27–13.31)

- **[13.27] Numbers in text versus numbers in notes** — Reference numbers are **superscript in the text**; in the
  notes themselves they are "normally **full size, not raised, and followed by a period**." In manuscripts,
  superscript numbers in both places (the word processor default) are perfectly acceptable. A **symbol** is
  superscript in the text but appears unraised and **without a following period** in the note.
  **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[13.28] Sequencing of note numbers and symbols** — Number consecutively from 1 **throughout each article and
  each new chapter** (not the whole book unless it has no internal divisions). Where only a handful of notes
  appear, use symbols: `*`, then `†`, then `‡`. Notes to tables and other nontextual matter are handled
  separately (3.77–81). **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[13.29] Placement of note number** — Put the number at the **end of a sentence or clause**, normally **after a
  quotation** (run-in or extract). "Relative to other punctuation, the number follows any punctuation mark
  **except for the dash, which it precedes**." **[MECHANICAL]**
  - ex: `"Good fiction is made of that which is real," wrote Ralph Ellison, "and reality is difficult to come by."¹`
  - ex: `It was the hour of "national paths" toward socialism;² but that expression …`
  - ex: `The bias was apparent in the Shotwell series³—and it must be remembered that Shotwell was a student of Robinson's.`
- **[13.30] Numbers with chapter and article titles and subheads** — A note number should **never** appear within
  or at the end of a **chapter title**. A note applying to a whole chapter is **unnumbered**, preferably at the
  foot of the chapter's first page, preceding numbered notes. Journals may use an asterisk at the end of the
  article title for article-wide notes. Note references at a subhead are numbered with the rest (some editors
  move them into the following text). **[JUDGMENT]**
  - A note number normally follows a closing parenthesis, but may go **inside** it if the note applies to a
    specific term: `… (the commonly acknowledged "marriage bar"¹), and men would be paid a "family wage."`
- **[13.31] Multiple citations and multiple note references** — **Never** place more than one note reference at the
  same spot; one note may contain several citations or comments (13.61). A note number **cannot reappear out of
  sequence** — repeat the substance under a new number, or use a checked cross-reference ("18. See note 3
  above."). Numbered-reference science styles (CSE) are the exception. **[MECHANICAL]** / **[JUDGMENT]**

### Shortened Citations (13.32–13.39)

- **[13.32] When to use shortened citations** — Shorten any citation already given in full — "should be shortened
  whenever possible" — to "reduce the bulk of documentation." Without a bibliography, **repeat the full citation
  the first time it appears in each new chapter**. A short form "should include enough information to remind
  readers of the full title or to lead them to the appropriate entry in the bibliography." **[JUDGMENT]**
- **[13.33] Basic structure of the short form** — Last name of the author + **main title**, "usually shortened if
  more than four words," + page.
  - ex: `4. Hendershot, When the News Broke, 165–66.`
  - ex: `5. Cavarzere, "Seventeenth-Century Italy," 42.`
  - ex: `6. Okabe, "Starving Beauties?," 55.`
- **[13.34] Cross-reference to the full citation** — When references are far apart and there is no full
  bibliography, add a checked cross-reference: `1. Miller, Quest, 81 (see chap. 1, n. 4).` Often better: repeat
  full details at first appearance in each chapter. **[JUDGMENT]**
- **[13.35] Short form for authors' names** — **Last name only** (of the author, or of the editor/translator if
  listed first). Add initials or full names **only** to distinguish authors who share a surname. Drop *ed.* /
  *trans.* Two authors: both. More than two: first + *et al.* **[MECHANICAL]**
  - ex: `3. King and Straub, Talisman, 433.` / `4. Kascakova et al., Katherine Mansfield, 101.`
- **[13.36] Short form for titles** — Use the key word(s) of the main title. An initial *A* or *The* is usually
  dropped from longer titles but may be kept if the title is short. **Never change the word order**:
  *Daily Notes of a Trip Around the World* → *Daily Notes* or *Around the World*, **not** *World Trip*. Titles of
  **four words or fewer are seldom shortened**. Keep the full title's typography (italics, or roman + quotation
  marks). In non-English titles, do not omit a word that governs the case ending of a retained word — ask a
  speaker of the language. **[JUDGMENT]**
  - ex: *The War Journal of Major Damon "Rocky" Gause* → *War Journal*; *Kriegstagebuch des Oberkommandos der
    Wehrmacht, 1940–1945* → *Kriegstagebuch*.
- **[13.37] Shortened citations versus "ibid."** — **18th-ed. position: prefer shortened citations to *ibid.*, and
  give *ibid.* a wide berth.** Chicago's reasons: *ibid.* "can obscure the identity of a source"; it saves no
  space, since a short form runs under a line; and in formats that link to one note at a time, "ibid. risks
  confusing the reader." Rules if you do use it: *ibid.* refers only to the **immediately preceding** note's single
  work; where that is not the case a short form is required; with a short form **repeat the page even if
  identical**, with *ibid.* an identical page is **not** repeated; capitalize at the start of a note and follow
  with a period.
  **[MECHANICAL]** (format) / **[JUDGMENT]** (referent)
  - ex: `3. Carson, Silent Spring, 18.` or `3. Ibid.`; `4. Ibid., 24–26.`
  - ex: successive shortened forms may drop to author only — `2. Carson, 18.` or `2. Silent Spring, 18` — "though
    the fuller form is usually preferred"; an author-only reference "is often the best choice within one note in
    successive references to the same work."
- **[13.38] "Idem"** — *Idem* ("the same," sometimes *id.*) has been used instead of a repeated author; it "is
  rarely used anymore except in legal references," where *id.* replaces *ibid.* (14.176). **Chicago discourages
  both *idem* and *ibid.* and uses short forms instead (13.33).** **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[13.39] "Op. cit." and "loc. cit."** — "Have rightly fallen into disuse"; a reader meeting "Wells, op. cit.,
  10" in note 95 must hunt back to note 2 — "or, worse still, find that two works by Wells have been cited."
  **Chicago disallows both**; use the short-title form (13.36). **[MECHANICAL]**

### Commentary and Quotations in Notes (13.40–13.45)

- **[13.40] Citations plus commentary in a note** — Put the **source first**, then a **period**, then the
  commentary. Such comments as "emphasis mine" go in parentheses. **[JUDGMENT]**
  - ex: `1. Shakespeare, Julius Caesar, act 3, sc. 1. Caesar's claim of constancy should be taken with a grain of salt.`
  - ex: `2. Little, "Norms of Collegiality," 330 (my italics).`
- **[13.41] Quotation within a note** — The source "normally follows the terminal punctuation of the quotation" and
  need **not** be parenthesized (parentheses would force existing parentheses to brackets, 6.107). Long
  quotations are set off as extracts in notes as in text; in notes, **more than three lines of poetry** should be
  set off. **[JUDGMENT]**
- **[13.42] Substantive notes** — Discursive notes may merely amplify the text and cite nothing; they may augment
  **any** system, including author-date (13.128). When a source is needed, treat it as in 13.41 or, if brief and
  already cited in full, put it parenthetically: `… in Language and Myth (59–62) …`. **[JUDGMENT]**
- **[13.43] Paragraphing within long notes** — Avoid very long **footnotes** (page-makeup problems, 13.47); long
  **endnotes** should be broken into paragraphs "as an aid to reading" — but first ask whether the note would be
  better shortened or partly folded into the text. **[JUDGMENT]**
- **[13.44] Footnotes that break across pages** — In print, break "in mid-sentence lest readers miss the end of
  the note," with a short rule above the continued part; this is a typesetting matter — at manuscript stage let
  the word processor break as it will. **[MECHANICAL]** (production only)
- **[13.45] "See" and "cf."** — Notes often invite further reading. Keep the distinction: **see** = consult;
  **cf.** = "compare" or "see, by way of comparison" — *cf.* never means "see." Neither is italicized in notes
  (but *see* **is** italicized in indexes, 15.22). **[JUDGMENT]**
  - ex: `1. For further discussion of this problem, see Jones, Conflict, 49.`
  - ex: `2. Others disagree with my position; cf. Fisher and Ury, Getting to Yes, 101–3.`

### Footnotes Versus Endnotes (13.46–13.51)

- **[13.46] Overview** — Footnotes sit at the foot of the page; endnotes at the end of an article, chapter, or
  book (usually the back of the book; at the end of the chapter in multiauthor books whose chapters may be sold
  separately). The publisher decides placement. Use the word processor's note-making function so numbering
  updates automatically. **[JUDGMENT]**
- **[13.47] Footnotes — pros and cons** — Readers of print "usually prefer footnotes for ease of reference,"
  especially when notes are closely integrated and interesting. Limits are page makeup (a basic requirement:
  a footnote must at least **begin** on the page it is referenced from) and appearance — "a page consisting
  almost exclusively of footnotes is daunting for many readers." Because they sit close to the text, footnotes
  may omit an author's name already given in the text (cf. 13.51). **[JUDGMENT]**
- **[13.48] Endnotes — pros and cons** — They pose no page-makeup problems and keep pages unintimidating, so
  publishers' marketing staff may prefer them for general readers. "Authors still need to aim for a healthy
  balance between text and notes (i.e., by resisting the temptation to include an excessive number of discursive
  notes)" — readers may be "disappointed to find a third or more of a book devoted to endnotes." The main
  drawback is finding a particular note; informative running heads help. **[JUDGMENT]**
- **[13.49] Endnote placement** — Group chapter endnotes in the end matter, **after the text and appendixes and
  before the bibliography**. Main heading "Notes"; each chapter's notes introduced by a subhead giving the
  chapter number and/or title. Where chapters are separately authored or sold, notes go at the end of each
  chapter; in journals, at the end of each article under a subhead "Notes." **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[13.50] Running heads for endnotes** — Where notes occupy more than two or three pages, run running heads
  giving the **text pages** the notes refer to ("Notes to Pages 123–125"; singular "Note to Page 23"). Do not
  abbreviate the second number; the rule for deriving the numbers is given in full (first and last notes
  *beginning* on the page → their text references). Such heads can only be set at page proofs and count as
  author's alterations. **[MECHANICAL]** (production)
- **[13.51] Special considerations for endnotes** — Footnotes may omit an author or title given in the text;
  **endnotes should repeat** the author (at least the surname) and title, "since at least some readers may have
  forgotten whether the note number was 93 or 94." "It is particularly annoying to arrive at the right place in
  the endnotes only to find another ibid." Consolidate instead: `1. This and the preceding four quotations are
  all from Hamlet, act 1, sc. 4.` / `2. … (Mariner Books, 2022). Further citations of this work are given in the
  text.` (the second device only if the source is clear from the text). **[JUDGMENT]**

### Two Sets of Notes (13.52–13.54)

- **[13.52] Endnotes plus footnotes** — In heavily documented works, split **source citations (numbered
  endnotes)** from **substantive notes (symbols as footnotes)**: `*`, `†`, `‡`, then doubled `**`, `††`, `‡‡`.
  "Almost always beneficial for readers, who might otherwise turn to the endnotes hoping for substantive
  commentary only to find a source citation." **[MECHANICAL]** (symbol order)
- **[13.53] Footnotes plus author-date citations** — Avoid the cumbersomeness of 13.52 by using **author-date for
  sources** and numbered notes only for substantive comments (13.128); numbered notes may themselves contain
  parenthetical author-date citations. **[JUDGMENT]**
- **[13.54] Editor's or translator's notes plus author's notes** — Distinguish added notes either by appending
  "—Ed." or "—Trans." after the final punctuation (usually with no space) or by enclosing the whole note, minus
  its number, in square brackets; a short editorial comment inside an author's note also goes in square
  brackets (6.105). If there are only a few, use symbols as footnotes above the numbered notes.
  **[MECHANICAL]**
  - ex: `1. Millicent Cliff was Norton Westermont's first cousin, although to the very last she denied it.—Ed.`
  - ex: `2. [The original reads gesungen; presumably gesunken is meant.]`

### Special Types of Notes (13.55–13.59)

- **[13.55] Unnumbered notes** — Footnotes without numbers or symbols **always precede** numbered notes on the
  page, most often on a chapter's opening page. Where footnotes are impossible, an unnumbered endnote goes
  **immediately before note 1** of the chapter (e.g., under the heading "Epigraph") — "to be used with caution in
  book-length works, where readers are likely to miss it." **[MECHANICAL]** (order)
- **[13.56] Notes keyed to line numbers** — For verse and similar works, key notes to line numbers instead of note
  numbers; line numbers may be added at manuscript stage only if they will be identical in every format
  (prose cannot be numbered until typeset). Note numbers are removed or hidden at that stage. **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[13.57] Notes keyed to page numbers** — Trade books may **suppress note numbers** in the text and key notes to
  page number plus "the word or phrase being annotated"; the phrase should be "short and meaningful" and is
  usually taken from the sentence introducing the quoted or annotated content. Phrase and note may be separated
  by a colon, with quotation marks reserved for actual quotations; multiple notes on a page may be distinguished
  as `188n1`, `188n2`. Applied by the publisher at typesetting. **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[13.58] Source notes for previously published material** — In anthologies or largely new books with reprinted
  chapters, give the source in an **unnumbered footnote on the chapter's first printed page**, ahead of numbered
  notes (keep it a footnote even if other notes are endnotes; some rights holders require it). For in-copyright
  material include original title, publisher or journal, date, pages or other locators, and — "very important" —
  mention of **permission from the copyright owner**; add a copyright notice if requested. Wording may be
  adjusted for consistency "as long as proper credit is given" (cf. 3.33).
  - ex: `Reprinted with permission from Steven Shapin, The Scientific Revolution (University of Chicago Press, 1996), 15–64.`
  - ex retitled: `Originally published as "Manet in His Generation: The Face of Painting in the 1860s," Critical Inquiry 19, no. 1 (1992): 22–69, © 1992 by The University of Chicago. All rights reserved. Reprinted by permission.`
  - ex public domain: `Reprinted from Ambler Thompson and Barry N. Taylor, Guide for the Use of the International System of Units (SI) (NIST, 2008), 38–39.`
- **[13.59] Biographical notes and acknowledgments** — In journals or multiauthor works a brief author note may
  appear as an unnumbered note on the first page of the article or chapter (or at its end); it is unnecessary if
  the work includes a list of contributors with affiliations. Acknowledgments may be appended to it.
  **[JUDGMENT]**

### Remedies for Excessive Annotation (13.60–13.64)

- **[13.60] Avoiding overlong notes** — Reduce or integrate lengthy discursive notes (13.47–48); move complicated
  tabular material, lists, and the like into an **appendix** (1.66), pointing to it from the text: "For a list of
  institutions involved, see appendix A." **[JUDGMENT]**
- **[13.61] Several citations in one note** — Group citations in one note to cut the number of note references:
  "The citations are separated by semicolons and must appear in the same order as the text material … to which
  they pertain. Take care to avoid any ambiguity as to what is documenting what." Omit given names already in
  the text. **[JUDGMENT]**
  - ex: `1. Sutton, "The Analysis of Free Verse Form …," Journal of Aesthetics and Art Criticism 18, no. 2 (December 1959): 241–54; Fussell, "Whitman's Curious Warble …," in The Presence of Walt Whitman, ed. R. W. B. Lewis (Columbia University Press, 1962); Coffman, …; and Lovell, …`
- **[13.62] Citing sources in the text rather than in the notes** — Parenthetical citation in the text is "another
  way to reduce the number of notes," and "can work well for a string of consecutive citations that refer to the
  same source" (with or without *ibid.*); see 12.75–83. **[JUDGMENT]**
- **[13.63] Abbreviations for frequently cited works** — Cite a frequently mentioned work by an abbreviation, with
  the full citation at first mention: `1. François Furet, The Passing of an Illusion …, trans. Deborah Furet
  (University of Chicago Press, 1999), 368 (hereafter cited in text as PI).` An **abbreviated title** differs from
  a **short title** (13.36): words may be abbreviated and word order changed (*Mass. Records*). More helpful with
  footnotes than with endnotes. **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[13.64] List of abbreviations** — With ten or more abbreviations (titles, manuscript collections, names), list
  them alphabetically in a separate section headed "Abbreviations," in the front matter if footnotes are used or
  in the end matter **preceding the endnotes**; include it in the table of contents. With only a few, they may
  open the endnotes or head the bibliography. Keep italics from the notes and bibliography in the abbreviated
  forms. **[MECHANICAL]**

## Bibliographies (13.65–13.73)

### Overview (13.65–13.68)

- **[13.65] Relationship of bibliographies to notes** — Not every annotated work needs a bibliography, since full
  details can live in the notes. But a full alphabetical bibliography gives "an overview of the sources and
  therefore an indication of the scope of an author's research" and "can serve as a convenient key to shortened
  forms of the notes" (13.18, 13.32). **[JUDGMENT]**
- **[13.66] Format and placement** — One **single alphabetical list** by authors' last names (or by title, or by a
  descriptive phrase, if no author or editor is given) — "the most common and usually the most reader-friendly
  form." Place it at the end of the work, **preceding the index**; in multiauthor books and textbooks each chapter
  may carry a brief list. **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[13.67] Dividing a bibliography into sections** — Divide "only if doing so would make the reader's job
  significantly easier": for manuscript/archival material that will not sort alphabetically; to separate kinds of
  works (an author's works vs. works about the author); or for a guide to further reading. Add a **headnote** at
  the start and an explanatory subhead to each section. **No source may appear in more than one section.**
  **[JUDGMENT]**
- **[13.68] Kinds of bibliographies** — Choose and **label** correctly:
  1. **Full bibliography** — all substantial works cited, excluding personal communications (14.111) and works
     mentioned but not relied on (13.5); some relevant consulted works may be added. Heading *Bibliography*, or
     *Works Cited* / *Literature Cited* if nothing extra is included.
  2. **Selected bibliography** — if not all works are listed, "the title must so indicate": *Selected
     Bibliography* (**preferred over *Select Bibliography*)** or, if very short, *Suggested Readings* / *Further
     Readings*; a headnote must explain the principles of selection.
  3. **Annotated bibliography** — annotations follow the publication details (in brackets if only a few entries
     are annotated) or start a new line, often indented. Preferred over a bibliographic essay.
  4. **Bibliographic essay** — literature treated discursively, so no alphabetizing and subject divisions are
     free; may accompany a bibliography and should then come **first**; if there is no bibliography the essay
     "must include full facts of publication."
  - **List of works by one author** — titled *Published Works [of X]* or *Writings [of X]*, arranged
    **chronologically**, with dates as subheads where several titles share a year.
  **[JUDGMENT]**

### Arrangement of Entries (13.69–13.71)

- **[13.69] Alphabetical order for bibliography entries** — Index rules (15.64–101) apply, with these
  modifications. "Chicago now recommends the **word-by-word** system but will accept the **letter-by-letter**
  system." Word-by-word: *Fernan Gémez, Fernando* precedes *Fernandez, Angelines*; letter-by-letter reverses them.
  Word processors "will generally not produce a perfectly sorted list for either system" — correct by hand, and
  adjust entries beginning with a numeral, punctuation, or a 3-em dash. **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[13.70] Alphabetical order for titles by the same author** — In notes-and-bibliography, works by one author are
  listed **alphabetically by title** (unlike author-date, 13.112); an initial *The*, *A*, or *An* is ignored;
  when an entry has both a chapter/article title and a book/periodical title, the **first** title governs. All
  works by the same person(s) in the same order are kept together whatever the role (*author*, *ed.*, *trans.*),
  "regardless of the added abbreviation."
  - ex: *A Grand Illusion?* → *Reappraisals* → *Resistance and Revolution* (ed.) under Judt.
- **[13.71] Entries with more than one author** — A **single-author entry precedes** a multiauthor entry beginning
  with the same name; only the first author is inverted. Successive entries sharing a first author are ordered by
  the **coauthors'** surnames (however many there are). Entries whose authors share a surname are ordered by the
  first-listed authors' **first names**.
  - ex: *Kogan, Herman.* … then *Kogan, Herman, and Lloyd Wendt.*
  - ex: *Brooks, Daniel R., and Deborah A. McLennan* before *Brooks, Daniel R., and E. O. Wiley*.

### The 3-Em Dash for Repeated Names in a Bibliography (13.72–13.73)

- **[13.72] Reasons to avoid** — **18th-ed. change: Chicago no longer recommends the 3-em dash** to replace the
  same author(s) in consecutive entries; **repeat the names** (13.69–71). The dash "effectively hides authors'
  names and can obscure entries from bibliographic databases and from readers using electronic formats, especially
  those who rely on assistive technology." **[MECHANICAL]** (and an accessibility issue)
- **[13.73] If you still use it** — The dash may stand in only for the **same two or more authors** (editors,
  translators included) in the **same order**, with no author present in one entry but absent from the other;
  alphabetization then falls to the **title**, and *ed.*/*trans.* never affect order. "A 3-em dash cannot be used
  for some authors but not others" — where coauthors differ, repeat the first author's name
  (`Brooks, Daniel R., and Deborah A. McLennan` … then `Brooks, Daniel R., and E. O. Wiley`, **not** dash + Wiley).
  Authors identical but listed in a different order **must be repeated**. Corporate authors follow the same rules.
  **[MECHANICAL]**

## Author's Name (13.74–13.86)

- **[13.74] Overview** — This section governs names in both systems and most source types in ch. 14; index entries
  are inverted the same way (15.79–95). **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[13.75] Form of the name** — Give names **as they appear with the source** (title page, head of article).
  Permissible adjustments: spell out first names in place of initials; use the **same form, preferably the fuller
  one**, across an author's works ("Mary L. Jones" vs. "M. L. Jones" → one form); add middle initials to
  differentiate similar names; **omit degrees and affiliations**; **do not invert Eastern-order names** (family
  name first) in a bibliography or reference list; when in doubt, check the author or the publisher's website.
  **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[13.76] Authors preferring initials** — "Full names should not be supplied for authors who always use
  initials" — W. E. B. Du Bois, T. S. Eliot, M. F. K. Fisher, O. Henry (pseud.), P. D. James, C. S. Lewis,
  J. D. Salinger, H. G. Wells. Put a **space between initials** (exception: H.D., the pen name of Hilda Doolittle).
  Bracket-supplied names are rare and limited to authors known in both forms — R[onald] S. Crane. **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[13.77] One author** — Note: normal order (*David Shields*). Bibliography: inverted (*Shields, David*).
  **[MECHANICAL]**
  - ex: `1. David Shields, The Very Last Interview (New York Review Books, 2022), 49.`
- **[13.78] Two or more authors (or editors)** — List in the order given with the source; invert **only the first**,
  with a comma **both before and after** that author's given name or initials; "**use the conjunction *and* (not an
  ampersand)**." Three to six (previously two to ten): give all names in the bibliography. In a **note** only the
  first author + *et al.*, **with no intervening comma**. More than six: first three + *et al.* in the
  bibliography (rationale: brevity, while author metadata in databases preserves cited-by credit, 1.84, 1.101);
  in a work with no bibliography, list up to six in the first full note. **[MECHANICAL]**
  - ex: `1. Ichiro Kishimi and Fumitake Koga, The Courage to Be Disliked … (Atria Books, 2018), 257.`
    / `Kishimi, Ichiro, and Fumitake Koga. The Courage to Be Disliked … Atria Books, 2018.`
  - ex: `3. Beatriz Williams et al., All the Ways We Said Goodbye … (William Morrow, 2021), 301–2.`
- **[13.79] Authors sharing a family name** — Repeat the name even if the source does not: `1. Lee Child and
  Andrew Child, The Sentinel (Delacorte Press, 2020), 128.` → `2. Child and Child, The Sentinel, 129–30.`
  **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[13.80] Author's name in the title** — When the author's name appears in the title or subtitle (autobiography,
  collected writings), the **note may begin with the title**; the **bibliography entry must begin with the
  author's name** even though it repeats the title. **[JUDGMENT]**
  - ex: `1. The Critical Writings of Oscar Wilde: An Annotated Selection, ed. Nicholas Frankel (Harvard University Press, 2022), 200–201.`
    / `Wilde, Oscar. The Critical Writings of Oscar Wilde: An Annotated Selection. Edited by Nicholas Frankel. Harvard University Press, 2022.`
- **[13.81] No listed author** — Begin with the **title**; ignore an initial article in alphabetizing. "Although the
  use of *Anonymous* is generally to be avoided," it (or *Anon.*) may group several unattributed works — and if a
  work is explicitly attributed to "Anonymous," cite it that way. Known or guessed authorship goes in **brackets**,
  with a question mark if uncertain: `[James Hawkes?], A Retrospect of the Boston Tea-Party …`.
  **[MECHANICAL]** / **[JUDGMENT]**
- **[13.82] Pseudonyms** — Add *pseud.* in roman, **in brackets**, after the name when readers might not realize it
  is false: `AK Muckraker [pseud.]. …`; an initial *The*, *A*, *An* may be omitted; **omit *pseud.* in a text
  citation or a short note form**. A **widely used pseudonym is treated as the real name** (Eliot, George;
  Twain, Mark). The real name may follow in brackets: `hooks, bell [Gloria Jean Watkins].` If the real name is
  better known, use it, optionally with the pseudonym bracketed plus *pseud.* (`Brontë, Charlotte [Currer Bell,
  pseud.].`). **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[13.83] Cross-references for pseudonyms** — Italicize *See*: `Carter, Shawn. See Jay-Z.` With several
  pseudonyms, list under the real name with bracketed pseudonyms and cross-references from each pseudonym, or the
  reverse. **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[13.84] Alternative real names** — List each work under the name that appears with it — "unless the difference
  is merely the use of initials versus full names" (13.75). Optional cross-reference, placed **after** the other
  titles by that author: `Doniger, Wendy. See also O'Flaherty, Wendy Doniger.` A person discussed in the text who
  publishes under another name may get `Overstone, Lord. See Loyd, Samuel Jones.` Where an author no longer uses
  an earlier name (a deadname), "it may be best to cite under the current form of the name only."
  **[JUDGMENT]**
- **[13.85] Authors known by a given name** — Mononyms and authors known only by a given name are listed and
  alphabetized by that name, dropping "King," "Saint," and place identifiers ("of Hippo," "of England") and any
  fuller alternative unless needed for disambiguation: *Augustine*, *Elizabeth I*, *Virgil*. **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[13.86] Organization as author** — If a publication issued by an organization, association, or corporation
  carries no personal author's name on the title page, "the organization is listed as author in a bibliography,
  **even if it is also given as publisher**" (but cf. 13.81).
  - ex: `University of Chicago Press. The Chicago Manual of Style. 18th ed. University of Chicago Press, 2024.`

## Title of Work (13.87–13.101)

- **[13.87] Scope** — This section governs titles in citations for most source types; for capitalization,
  punctuation, wording, and relationship to surrounding text see 8.157–204, most of which "applies equally to
  source citations." **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[13.88] Italics versus quotation marks** — In citations as in running text: **italics for books and journals**,
  and for newspapers, blogs, movies, video games, paintings, and similar whole works; **quotation marks for
  subsections of larger works** — chapter and article titles, poems in a collection. **Neither** for some types,
  e.g. a book series or a website. **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[13.89] Capitalization** — English-language titles are set in **title case (headline style)**: capitalize the
  first and last words of title and subtitle and all other major words (8.160; hyphenated compounds 8.162).
  - ex: *Quiet: The Power of Introverts in a World That Can't Stop Talking*; "Shooting an Elephant."
- **[13.90] Some permissible changes** — Preserve the original spelling, hyphenation, and punctuation, with these
  exceptions: **words in full capitals → upper- and lowercase** (except initialisms and acronyms); apply **title
  case** (but see 13.100); **an ampersand may be changed to *and* (editorial discretion)**; keep numbers spelled
  out or in numerals as in the original (*Twelfth Century* or *12th Century*) unless consistency is a good reason;
  a mangled ordinal may be normalized (the book's example is OCR'd as *12"*, read *12th*); punctuation between
  title and subtitle may be added or adjusted (13.91–93). **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[13.91] Subtitles and the colon** — "A colon is used to separate the main title from the subtitle (**even if no
  colon appears in the source itself**)." Space after the colon; italicize the colon in an italicized title; the
  subtitle **always begins with a capital**. The generic subtitle *A Novel* "can usually be omitted, even if it
  appears on the title page." English-language publications need not adopt the European period between title and
  subtitle for non-English titles. **[MECHANICAL]**
  - ex: `Wirth, Eileen. The Women Who Built Omaha: A Bold and Remarkable History. University of Nebraska Press, 2022.`
- **[13.92] Two subtitles** — Colon before the first, **semicolon** before the second; the second begins with a
  capital. An em dash is "usually considered part of a title or subtitle."
  - ex: `Sereny, Gitta. Cries Unheard: Why Children Kill; The Story of Mary Bell. Metropolitan Books / Henry Holt, 1999.`
  - ex: `Silver, Nate. The Signal and the Noise: Why So Many Predictions Fail—but Some Don't. Penguin Books, 2015.`
- **[13.93] Double titles with "or"** — Prefer the punctuation of the title page. Where there is none (or it cannot
  be consulted), use the simple form: *The Tempest, or The Enchanted Island*. Preserve *Moby-Dick; or, The Whale*
  when that is the original; modern film titles may keep a colon and add a comma: *Dr. Strangelove, or: How I
  Learned to Stop Worrying and Love the Bomb*. **Always capitalize the first word after *or*.** **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[13.94] "And other stories" and such** — When a book title ends *and other stories*, *and other poems*, or the
  like, put the **main title in quotation marks**; **no comma** follows the main title unless the title page has
  one. (**18th-ed. change:** previous editions added a comma and omitted the quotation marks.)
  - ex: `1. Norman Maclean, "A River Runs Through It" and Other Stories (University of Chicago Press, 1976), 104.`
- **[13.95] Dates in titles** — When not introduced by a preposition ("from 1920 to 1945"), dates in a title or
  subtitle "are set off by commas, even if differentiated only by type style or a new line." Retain a colon that
  appears in the original (but see 13.92). **Do not add commas to non-English titles before dates** (11.9).
  **[MECHANICAL]**
  - ex: `Overy, Richard. Blood and Ruins: The Last Imperial War, 1931–1945. Viking, 2022.`
- **[13.96] Titles and quoted terms within titles** — A book or other work's title appearing inside an italicized
  title goes in **quotation marks**, "even if it would normally appear in italics when not enclosed in another
  title" (*Annotations to "Finnegans Wake"*). A term normally quoted inside a title that is itself in quotation
  marks takes **single** quotation marks (12.30, 6.11); retain both sets in short citations.
  - ex: `5. Lewis, "'Tis a Misfortune," 32.`
- **[13.97] Italicized terms within titles** — Species names, ship names, and other terms normally italicized **may
  remain in italics inside an italicized title** rather than switching to roman — "this departure from previous
  editions is intended to make cited titles more legible" (see also 8.175). The second element of a binomial stays
  lowercase in a title. Italicized terms (including titles) inside a roman-type article or chapter title **stay
  italic**. **[MECHANICAL]**
  - ex: `Zimmer, Carl. Microcosm: E. coli and the New Science of Life. Vintage Books, 2009.`
- **[13.98] Question marks and exclamation points in titles** — When the main title ends with **?** or **!**, **add
  no colon** before a subtitle — but **retain the colon** when the ?/! is followed by a closing quotation mark.
  Keep any punctuation the surrounding text requires; in a shortened title, a ?/! "that would not make sense out
  of context may be dropped." When a title ending in ? or ! would otherwise be followed by a period, **omit the
  period**. **[MECHANICAL]**
  - ex: `1. Yogi Berra, What Time Is It? You Mean Now? Advice for Life from the Zennest Master of Them All, with Dave Kaplan (Simon & Schuster, 2002), 63.` → short `7. Berra, What Time Is It?, 55–56.`
  - ex: `5. Edward Buscombe, "Injuns!": Native Americans in the Movies (Reaktion, 2006), 12.` → short `11. Buscombe, "Injuns!," 114–15.`
  - ex: `3. … "Diversity and Distribution of Stream Bryophytes: Does pH Matter?," Freshwater Science 33, no. 3 (2014): 778.`
- **[13.99] Older titles and very long titles** — Titles from the eighteenth century or earlier **may retain**
  original punctuation, spelling, and capitalization (except whole words in capitals, which get an initial capital
  only). Very long titles may be shortened in a note or bibliography with **bracketed ellipses `[. . .]`**, which
  signal that the omission is the author's; at the end of a title the bracketed ellipsis is followed by a period.
  **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[13.100] Non-English titles** — **Sentence case is strongly recommended** (8.159): capitalize the first word of
  the title or subtitle and any word the original language would capitalize. Writers unfamiliar with the language
  "should not attempt to alter capitalization without expert help." **Retain an initial definite article** (*Le*,
  *Der*) because it may govern the inflection of the next word — and, unlike *The*, it **counts in alphabetizing**
  by title (13.70). Months and the equivalents of *no.* or *pt.* are usually given in English. **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[13.101] Translated titles** — An English translation, when needed, "follows the original title and is enclosed
  in brackets, without italics or quotation marks," and is capitalized in **sentence case** regardless of style
  (in running text use parentheses, 11.11). If only the English translation is given, **name the original
  language**: `The Zemstvo Liberal Movement … [in Russian]`; `"The Vicissitudes of the Giant Panda, Ailuropoda
  melanoleuca (David)." [In Chinese.]`
  Both original and translation may be listed when both matter: `… Translated by Stephen Snyder as The Memory
  Police (Vintage Books, 2020).` **[MECHANICAL]**

## Author-Date: Basic Format (13.102–13.104)

- **[13.102] The system** — A variation on notes and bibliography: sources are cited **in the text**, usually in
  parentheses, by **author's last name + year of publication**, with full details in a reference list "usually
  titled *References* or *Works Cited*." The one structural difference from a bibliography: **the year appears
  immediately after the author's name**, which "makes it easy to follow a text citation to the corresponding full
  source."
  - ex text: `… (Lampel et al. 2000; Hutter 2011). … (Bissell 2011).`
  - ex list: `Bissell, Tom. 2011. Extra Lives: Why Video Games Matter. Vintage Books.`
  - ex list: `Lampel, Joseph, Theresa Lant, and Jamal Shamsie. 2000. "Balancing Act: Learning from Organizing Practices in Cultural Industries." Organization Science 11 (3): 263–69.`
- **[13.103] Abbreviations** — As in a bibliography, **noun forms are abbreviated** (*editor*, *translator*,
  *volume*, *edition*) and **verb forms are spelled out** (*edited by*, *translated by*); **in a text citation all
  such terms are omitted**. **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[13.104] Page numbers** — Parenthetical citations may include specific page numbers (13.117). A reference-list
  entry gives the article's **beginning and ending pages** but omits most other page numbers, "including for a
  chapter in a book" (13.109). **[MECHANICAL]**

## Author-Date: Examples and Variations (13.105–13.110)

- **[13.105] How the examples work** — Each example pairs a reference-list entry with a text citation; citations
  are shown parenthetically "though they do not always appear that way in practice" (13.122).
  **[JUDGMENT]**
- **[13.106] Book with single author or editor** — Invert in the list; **last name only** in the text. A specific
  passage takes a page or range **separated from the year by a comma** (and a comma between nonconsecutive pages).
  **No page numbers in the reference-list entry.** The text citation omits *ed.* **[MECHANICAL]**
  - ex: `Yu, Charles. 2020. Interior Chinatown. Pantheon Books.` / `(Yu 2020, 95–96)` / `(Yu 2020, 101, 103)`
  - ex: `Callard, Agnes, ed. 2020. On Anger. Boston Review.` / `(Callard 2020, 7)`
- **[13.107] Book with multiple authors** — Two authors: invert only the first; **both last names in the text**.
  More than two: up to **six** in the reference list, more than six → first **three** + *et al.*; in the text,
  **first author + et al.** **[MECHANICAL]**
  - ex: `Sterling, Terry Greene, and Jude Joffe-Block. 2021. Driving While Brown … University of California Press.` / `(Sterling and Joffe-Block 2021, 110)`
  - ex: `(Aubrecht et al. 2020, 44)`
- **[13.108] Book with author plus editor or translator** — **Do not abbreviate** *Edited by* / *Translated by*
  in the reference list.
  - ex: `García Márquez, Gabriel. 1988. Love in the Time of Cholera. Translated by Edith Grossman. Alfred A. Knopf.` / `(García Márquez 1988, 242–55)`
- **[13.109] Chapter in an edited book** — Chapter author, chapter title in quotation marks, editor, and *In*
  before the book title; **no chapter page range** in the entry (an 18th-ed. departure), page in the text
  citation.
  - ex: `Gould, Glenn. 1984. "Streisand as Schwarzkopf." In The Glenn Gould Reader, edited by Tim Page. Vintage Books.` / `(Gould 1984, 310)`
- **[13.110] Journal article** — Volume follows the italicized journal title **in roman with no intervening
  punctuation**; **issue number in parentheses** "unless a month or season of publication is included" (14.70);
  page in the text, article page range in the list **preceded by a colon**.
  - ex: `Kwon, Hyeyoung. 2022. "Inclusion Work: …" American Journal of Sociology 127 (6): 1818–59. https://doi.org/10.1086/720277.` / `(Kwon 2022, 1824)`

## Author-Date Reference Lists (13.111–13.114)

- **[13.111] Function and placement** — "In the author-date system, the reference list is the prime vehicle for
  documentation"; text citations "are merely pointers to the sources." Title it *References* or *Works Cited* and
  place it at the end, **preceding the index**. Multiauthor books and textbooks give each chapter its own list;
  **journal articles are always treated this way**. **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[13.112] Alphabetical arrangement** — Alphabetical by author, but — unlike a bibliography — multiple sources by
  the same author are ordered **chronologically (oldest to newest)**, not alphabetically by title. As in a
  bibliography, a single-author entry precedes a multiauthor entry that begins with the same author name, and
  successive entries sharing a first author are alphabetized by coauthors' surnames (13.71). **[MECHANICAL]**
  - ex: Judt 1989 (ed.), Judt 1996, Judt 2008; Mulvany 1990, 1993 (ed.), 2005.
- **[13.113] The 3-em dash in a reference list** — Chicago recommends **repeating the names**; those who must use
  the dash apply 13.73 but keep **chronological** order. "The 3-em dash cannot stand in for *ed.*" — in the Judt
  example the first work (edited) keeps its name and only the authored ones take the dash. **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[13.114] Same author(s), same year** — Two or more works by the same author in the same year "**must** be
  differentiated by the addition of a, b, and so forth," listed **alphabetically by title**, with text citations
  giving author/year/letter. If coauthors are listed in a different order, letters **cannot** be used. Undated and
  forthcoming works take a **hyphen**: *n.d.-a*, *n.d.-b*; *forthcoming-a*, *forthcoming-b*. **[MECHANICAL]**
  - ex: `Fogel, Robert William. 2004a. The Escape from Hunger …` / `2004b. "Technophysio Evolution …"` →
    `(Fogel 2004b, 218)`, `(Fogel 2004a, 45–46)`

## Author-Date Text Citations (13.115–13.128)

- **[13.115] Agreement with the reference list** — "For each author-date citation in the text, there must be a
  corresponding entry in the reference list **under the same name and date**." Ensuring this — and the accuracy of
  the reference — is the **author's** responsibility; a page reference in a text citation "must fall within the
  range of pages given for the article in the reference list entry." Editors cross-check and query discrepancies.
  **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[13.116] Basic form** — Last (family) name + year, "**no punctuation** appears between author and date";
  "author" may be one or more persons, an institution, or editors/translators/compilers, and such abbreviations as
  *ed.* or *trans.* are omitted. Two or more sources in one citation are separated by **semicolons**. Where
  different authors share a surname, add initials (or a given name if needed): `(A. Miller 2020)`, `(D. Miller
  2022)`. **[MECHANICAL]**
  - ex: `(Ma 2022)`, `(Braverman and Johnson 2020)`, `(Ma 2022; Braverman and Johnson 2020)`
- **[13.117] Page numbers and other locators** — A specific page, section, equation, or other division follows the
  date **after a comma**; *vol.* is used for a volume alone; **volume + page takes only a colon**; *n* means note
  (14.56). For sources without page or section numbers, cite a heading: `(Heliman 2017, under "The Battleground")`.
  Some journals omit page numbers for other journal articles except in a direct quotation or paraphrase.
  **[MECHANICAL]**
  - ex: `(Piaget 1980, 74)` · `(LaFree 2010, 413, 417–18)` · `(Claussen 2015, para. 2.15)` or `(Claussen 2015, § 2.15)` · `(Johnson 1979, sec. 24)` · `(Fowler and Hoyle 1965, eq. 87)` · `(Hsu 2017, chap. 4)` · `(Garcia 1987, vol. 2)` / `(Garcia 1987, 2:345)` · `(Barnes 1998, 2:354–55, 3:29)` · `(Fischer and Siple 1990, 212n3)`
- **[13.118] Additional material** — A comment may ride inside the citation parentheses, **separated by a
  semicolon**: `(Mandolan 2024; t-tests are used here)`. **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[13.119] Relation to surrounding text** — Citations go "just before a mark of punctuation though need not be
  if the sentence would otherwise not require it." When the author's name is in the text, it **need not be
  repeated** in the parentheses; the **date should immediately follow the author's name, even in the possessive**
  — `Tufte's (2001) excellent book on chart design warns …`. **[JUDGMENT]** / **[MECHANICAL]** (possessive form)
  - ex: `Recent literature has examined long-run price drifts following initial public offerings (Ritter 1991; Loughran and Ritter 1995), stock splits (Ikenberry et al. 1996), …`
- **[13.120] Direct quotations** — The citation may **precede** the quotation "especially if such a placement allows
  the date to appear with the author's name": `As Edward Tufte (2001, 139) points out, "A graphical element may
  carry data information …".` For a **block quotation** with the source at the end, the opening parenthesis comes
  **after the final punctuation mark of the quoted material, and no period precedes or follows the closing
  parenthesis**. **[MECHANICAL]**
  - ex: `… and have taken a Golden Grouper. (Pinchot 1930, 123)`
- **[13.121] Consecutive citations to the same source** — When the same page (or range) is cited more than once in
  one paragraph, put one parenthetical after the last reference or at the **end of the paragraph, preceding the
  final period**. When different pages of the same source are cited, give the full citation at the **first**
  reference and **page numbers only** thereafter.
  - ex: `… "his cheeks blotch indignantly, painted by jealousy and rage" (Chaston 2000, 47). On the other hand, his mother's mask never changes, her "even-tanned good looks" (56), "burnished visage" (101), and "air-brushed confidence" (211) …`
- **[13.122] Syntactic considerations** — A citation "is a form of bibliographic shorthand that corresponds to a
  fully cited work; **it does not refer to a person**." Avoid "in Smith 1999" except inside a parenthetical;
  prefer "in Smith (1999)" or "in Smith's (1999) study" — "to help readers identify the source citation." Use
  **square brackets** for parentheses inside a parenthetical reference: `(see, e.g., Haviland [2003, 767] on how
  US courts disregard …)`. **[JUDGMENT]**
- **[13.123] Works with more than two authors** — First author + *et al.* (**18th-ed. change:** previously more
  than three); *et al.* is **not italicized**. If two references of the same year would both abbreviate to the
  same form with different coauthors, cite the first two (or as many as needed) + *et al.*:
  `(Schonen, Baker, et al. 2024)` / `(Schonen, Brooks, et al. 2024)`, or add a shortened title in commas:
  `(Schonen et al., "Tilting at Windmills," 2024)`. **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[13.124] Multiple references** — Separate with **semicolons**; order by what is being cited and in what order,
  or by relative importance, else alphabetically or chronologically — "unless the order is prescribed by a
  particular journal style, the decision is the author's." Additional works by the same author(s) are cited **by
  date only**, separated by commas except where page numbers are required; this applies with *et al.* too.
  References prefaced by "see also" **follow** all others. **[MECHANICAL]**
  - ex: `(Armstrong and Malacinski 1989; Beigl 1989; Pickett and White 1985)`
  - ex: `(Whittaker 1967, 1975; Wiens 1989a, 1989b)` / `(Wong 1999, 328; 2000, 475; Garcia 1998, 67)` / `(Schonen et al. 2023, 2024)`
  - ex: `(Guest et al. 2006; see also Stolle et al. 2008; Rahn et al. 2009)`
- **[13.125] Title rather than author** — If author/editor/translator is unknown, the entry begins with the title;
  text citations then refer to the **title**, in a short form of **up to the first four words** (not counting an
  initial article) that **must include the first word** other than an initial article.
  - ex: `(Stanze in lode della donna brutta 1547)` or `(Stanze 1547)`; `(True and Sincere Declaration 1610)`
- **[13.126] Pseudonyms** — Indicate exactly as in a bibliography (13.82); the text citation should refer to the
  **first-listed name** and **omit *pseud.* entirely**: `Centinel [pseud.]. 1981. …` → `(Centinel 1981)`.
  **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[13.127] Organization as author** — To keep parenthetical citations short, an organization may be cited by an
  **abbreviation**, in which case the entry **must be alphabetized under the abbreviation**, not under the spelled
  out name: `ISO (International Organization for Standardization). 1997. …` → `(ISO 1997)`. **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[13.128] Author-date with notes** — Where notes supplement author-date, source citations **inside notes are
  treated exactly as in the text**; notes "should be reserved for substantive commentary and should not be taken
  as permission to move parenthetical text citations into the notes." (For notes with legal-style citations, see
  14.172.) **[JUDGMENT]**

### Facts of publication

| Rule · type | Rule (with the chapter's own examples) |
|---|---|
| **[14.29] Publisher and date** | Facts of publication = **publisher + year**; in parentheses in a note, bare in a bibliography; date follows publisher after a comma: `(Hogarth Press, 1927)` / `Coates, Ta-Nehisi. *The Water Dancer*. One World, 2019.` **[MECHANICAL]**|
| **[14.30] Place of publication** | **No place is required for books published since 1900**; "authors should resist" including one. If included: `Paris: Shakespeare and Company, 1922.` — city + colon; add state/country when ambiguous (`Ithaca, NY`). |
| **[14.31] Books published before 1900** | Give **place + date, omit the publisher**: `(Salisbury, 1766)`; `Madrid, 1605–15`. If a publisher is given for some reason, keep the city and colon: `London: T. Egerton, 1811.` |
| **[14.32] Preferred form of publisher's name** | Full or somewhat abbreviated — shorter preferred; reflect the name **at the date of publication**, not today's name. |
| **[14.33] Omit from publisher names** | Initial `The`; `Inc.`, `Ltd.`, `S.A.`, `Co.`, `& Co.`, `Publishing Co.` (even spelled-out `Company`). Keep given names/initials, `Sons`, `Brothers`, and usually `Books`. `Press` may go with `Pergamon`, `Ecco`; keep it for `Free Press`, `New Press`, and **always for a university press**; `University` → `Univ.` only if consistent. `Houghton Mifflin` not `Houghton Mifflin Co.`; `Little, Brown` not `Little, Brown & Co.` **[MECHANICAL]** |
| **[14.34] `and` vs. `&`** | Follow the title page; make it consistent if sources disagree; never use `&` in a non-English name (`Duncker und Humblot`). Serial comma usually omitted before `&`, kept before `and`; exception `Farrar, Straus and Giroux`. **[MECHANICAL]** |
| **[14.35] Non-English publishers** | Translate **nothing**: `Fondo de Cultura Económica, 2019`; `Presses Universitaires de France, 2024`; drop `GmbH` and the like. Capitalize per the source, converting full caps to title case. |
| **[14.36] Self-published** | `1. Jamie McGuire, *Sins of the Immortal*, Providence Series 5 (pub. by author, 2021), Kindle.` / `Hammel, Sara. *The Strong Ones*. Published by the author, 2021.` — Chicago **now prefers "published by the author" to "self-published"**; omit the platform (Amazon) and any place. **[18th-ed. change]** |
| **[14.37–14.38] Imprints** | Cite the **imprint** (usually the first-listed name): `Soyinka, Wole. *The Interpreters*. | **[14.39–14.40] Copublication and distribution** | Cite only the publisher "more relevant to the users of the citation"; if both appear on the title page, separate with a **semicolon** (record both dates if they differ). | **[14.41–14.42] Date of publication** | **Year only** for books — no month or day; take it from the copyright page; the most recent of several copyright dates is the publication date. Disregard `53rd impression` and `Copyright renewed 1980` — a new printing is not a new edition. |
| **[14.43] Multiyear works** | Give the span (`1951–63`); if unfinished, first date + en dash (`1988–`); a single volume cited → that volume's date only. AD: `Tillich, Paul. 1951–63. *Systematic Theology*. 3 vols.` · `(Tillich 1951–63, 1:133)` |
| **[14.44] No date** | `(Boston, n.d.)`, `(Edinburgh, [1750?])` or `Edinburgh, n.d., ca. 1750`. AD: `Smith, John. n.d. *Title*.` · `(Smith, n.d.)` — `n.d.` stays **lowercase** even after a period. | **[14.45] Forthcoming** | `(Publisher, forthcoming)`; AD `Faraday, Carry. Forthcoming. "Protean Photography." …` but `(Faraday, forthcoming)` — capitalized in the list, lowercase and comma-preceded in text. ### Page, volume, and other locators

- **[14.46] Use Arabic numerals for all divisions** — volumes, chapters, sections — "regardless of the way the numerals appear in the works cited"; the exception is pages printed with lowercase roman numerals in front matter (`xvi–xvii`). Leave numbers inside titles as is. **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[14.47] Give first and last page, never `ff.`** — `14.46–57`, `170–71`; nonconsecutive pages take commas: `172, 174`. AD identical: `(Hossenfelder 2022, 170–71)`. **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[14.48] `ff.` and `passim` are to be discouraged** — Use `ff.` only when no final number can usefully be given: `220ff.` (no space, period after). `passim` only after a stated range containing more than three or four references (`324–32 passim`); no period, no italics. Never `140f.` → use `140–41`. **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[14.49] Abbreviate and lowercase locators** — `p./pp.`, `vol.`, `pt.`, `chap.`, `bk.`, `sec.`, `n./nn.`, `no.`, `app.`, `fig.`; lowercase unless following a period, never italic; plurals add `s` except `p.`/`n.`. **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[14.50] Omit `p.`/`pp.` when the number is clearly a page** — `*Charlotte's Web*, 75–76`; add them where other numerals threaten ambiguity: `*Complete Poems of Michelangelo*, p. 89, lines 135–36`. Don't strip them if the author used them consistently. **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[14.51] Omit `vol.` when volume is followed by page** — `*The Complete Tales of Henry James*, 10:122` — colon, **no space**. Comma precedes the volume number except with periodicals and classical references. **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[14.52] Page numbers are usually unnecessary in bibliographies** — and "no longer required for chapters" (14.8); if a chapter/section number is given in a note, pages may be dropped. Never give a book's total page count. **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[14.53–14.54] Early-book locators** — signature symbol + leaf number + `r`/`v`: `G6v`, `G6r–7v`; folios: `176r`, `231v`, `232r–v`, `fol. 176r`, `fols. 176r–177v`; unnumbered pages may be counted and bracketed.
- **[14.55] Don't abbreviate `line` as `l.`** — too easily confused with `1`/`11`; write `lines 1–4` or omit when context is clear. **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[14.56] Notes: page + `n` + note number** — `72n`, `80n` (only note on the page); `192n23`, `201nn16–17`, `1:15n29`; a note spanning pages = `10–11n5`; with roman page numbers insert a space: `vii n3`. **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[14.57] Illustrations** — `fig.` may be abbreviated; `table`, `map`, `plate` are spelled out; page first, comma, then the number: `76, table 3.1`; `228, fig. 8.3`. **[MECHANICAL]**

## Periodicals (14.63–14.102)

- **[14.63] Periodical = journal, magazine, or newspaper** — Every citation needs author, article title, periodical title, issue information, page (where appropriate), and a URL or database name for online copies.
- **[14.65] Journal vs. magazine** — Journal = scholarly/professional, cited by **volume and date**; magazine = weekly/monthly, cited by **date alone**. If in doubt, use journal form when a volume number is easy to find.
- **[14.66] Punctuation skeleton** — In notes, **commas** separate author; "title"; *Periodical*; URL. In bibliographies, **periods** replace them. **Never use `in` between an article title and the periodical title** (`in` is only for chapters in books). **[MECHANICAL]**

## Manuscript collections (14.119–14.129)

- **[14.119] The governing principle (quoted from the *Guide to Federal Records*)** — "The most convenient citation for archives is one similar to that used for personal papers … Full identification of most unpublished material usually requires giving the title and date of the item, series title (if applicable), name of the collection, and name of the depository. … Whatever sequence is adopted, however, should be used consistently throughout the same work."
- **[14.120] Note cites the item; bibliography cites the collection** — In a note the **specific item** comes first; in a bibliography the entry is usually the **collection**, the collection's author(s), or the depository. Specific items appear in a bibliography only when a single item from a collection is cited. **[JUDGMENT]**
- **[14.121] Quotation marks only for specific titles** — `"Concerning a Court of Arbitration"` yes; generic names (`report`, `minutes`) no — capitalize them only if part of a formal heading on the manuscript. **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[14.122] Dates** — Months may be spelled out or abbreviated if consistent; `8 May 1945` "may improve readability" in letter/diary collections, though month-day-year is acceptable.
- **[14.123–14.125] Locators, terminology, depositories** (14.123 folios/pages, 14.124 papers/manuscripts, 14.125 depositories) — older manuscripts by signature or leaf (`fol.`), recent ones by page (`p./pp.` to avoid ambiguity); file/series numbers may be included. `papers` = `manuscripts`; `MS`/`MSS` and `TS` are acceptable; city/state rarely needed for major repositories.
- **[14.126] Collections of letters** — writer **to** recipient, then date, then collection: "The word *letter* is usually omitted … but other forms of communication (telegram, memorandum) are specified."
- **[14.127–14.128] Forms** — notes: `1. George Creel to Colonel House, 25 September 1918, Edward M. House Papers, Yale University Library.` · `3. Burton to Merriam, telegram, 26 January 1923, box 26, folder 17, Charles E. Merriam Papers, Special Collections Research Center, University of Chicago Library.` · `12. Daily Expenses, July 1787, images 7–8, George Washington Papers, Series 5: Financial Papers, 1750–96, Library of Congress, Washington, DC, https://www.loc.gov/item/mgw500032/.` — bibliography: `Kallen, Horace. Papers. YIVO Institute for Jewish Research, New York.` · `Egmont Manuscripts. Phillipps Collection. University of Georgia Library.` Short forms may trade consistency for convenience (`Baker to House, 1 November 1919, House Papers.`).
- **[14.128–14.129] Author-date** — no `n.d.` needed for collections; put item dates in the text: "Oglethorpe wrote to the trustees on January 13, 1733 (Egmont Manuscripts), to say…" But a one-item entry may begin with the writer and use `n.d.`: `Dinkel, Joseph. n.d. Description of Louis Agassiz … (Dinkel, n.d.)`
- **[14.129] Private collections** — replace the depository with `in the author's possession` or `private collection`; give no location.

## Legal and public documents (14.170–14.207)

- **[14.170] Use a legal stylebook, even in nonlegal work** — Chicago recommends *The Bluebook* or the *ALWD Guide* "for citing legal and public documents—including cases, constitutions, statutes, and other government documents—even in works with predominantly nonlegal subject matter." Most examples here follow *Bluebook*.
- **[14.171] Print preferred; authenticated online versions count as print** — an official or exact-copy online version (e.g., an unaltered PDF) may be treated as print; URLs may be appended last. Commercial databases (`Westlaw`, `Lexis`) → give the database name and any identifying number, or, for constitutions/statutes, currency information.
- **[14.172] Legal citations use notes only, no bibliography** — and a work using author-date that needs more than the occasional legal document should use **supplementary footnotes or endnotes**. A handful may be handled in the text: `In *NLRB v. Somerville Construction Co.* (206 F.3d 752 (7th Cir. 2000)), the court ruled that …`
- **[14.173] Typefaces depart from Chicago** — *Bluebook* italicizes article/chapter titles, introductory signals (`See`), case-history terms (`aff'd`), and procedural phrases (`In re`); **case names in citations are roman**, but case names in running text are italic. Constitutions and periodical names take caps/small caps in *Bluebook*; this section uses conventional caps or Chicago italics for simplicity.
- **[14.174] Locators** — first page follows the source name with no punctuation; specific pages follow a comma; sections use `§`/`¶` and `at` in roman type.
- **[14.175] Abbreviations and ordinals** — *Bluebook* uses `2d`/`3d` (not `2nd`/`3rd`) and capitalizes `No.`, `Sess.` **in citations**; spell out `chapter`, `part`, `article`, `section`, `paragraph` in running text, but keep `v.` and abbreviations like `Co.` in case names. **[MECHANICAL]**
- **[14.176] Short forms** — case names reduced to one party, statute/document + section, treaty name, or `id.` (italic); if only a few documents are cited, use the full form every time.

## Cross-cutting mechanical checks (any citation)

- **Commas in notes, periods in the bibliography** — same elements, different separators (14.66, 14.73). **[MECHANICAL]**
- **`in` only for parts of books**, never between an article title and a periodical title (14.66). **[MECHANICAL]**
- **En dashes in all ranges** (`621–43`, `1962–64`, `114–15`); no `ff.` with a known end (14.47–48). **[MECHANICAL]**
- **Volume:page with no space** (`4:243`, `10:122`, `70:782`); when parenthetical issue data intervenes, a colon **plus a space** (`Ethics 132, no. 3 (2022): 621–43`); a comma before a page that follows an issue number (`Diogenes, no. 25, 84–117`) (14.51, 14.70, 14.74).
- **Lowercase locator abbreviations** unless they follow a period (`vol.`, `no.`, `chap.`, `n.`, `fig.`) (14.49). **[MECHANICAL]**
- **`n.d.` always lowercase**; `forthcoming` capitalized only in a reference list (14.44–45). **[MECHANICAL]**
- **Seasons are capitalized in citations, lowercase in running text** (14.70). **[MECHANICAL]**
- **Never give a book's total page count; never cite a Bible page number** (14.52, 14.138). **[MECHANICAL]**
- **No city of publication for post-1900 books**; pre-1900 → place + date, no publisher (14.30–31). **[MECHANICAL]**
- **Website titles roman; blog and periodical titles italic; article and chapter titles in quotation marks** (14.67–69, 14.103). **[MECHANICAL]**
- **Not separately illustrated above** (same forms as their neighbours): **[14.23]** parts of individual volumes (cite like a chapter; add `3:181` when the volume has no title); **[14.64]** the periodical data checklist; **[14.182]** legislative abbreviations (`Cong.`, `H.`, `S.`); **[14.194], [14.197], [14.198], [14.201]** Canadian and UK reference works, Canadian and UK archives, and UK parliamentary publications.

**18th-edition changes flagged in the text**

- Chapter and contribution **page ranges no longer required** in bibliography entries (14.8, 14.9, 14.12).
- Author-date lists **no longer cross-reference** individual components of a multiauthor book to the whole (14.10).
- **City of publication no longer required** for books published since 1900 (14.30).
- **"Published by the author"** now preferred to "self-published" (14.36).
- **Parentheses dropped** around descriptive information for unpublished manuscripts and conference presentations (14.114, 14.115).
- **`s.v.` no longer recommended**; use `under` (14.130).
- New material: **Indigenous sources and Oral Traditions** (14.136–137), **AI-generated content** (14.112), **datasets and databases** (14.157), **podcasts/apps** (14.168–169), **social media** (14.106).
